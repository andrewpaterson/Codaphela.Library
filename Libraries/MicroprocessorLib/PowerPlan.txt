Power-up in the simulation
==========================

Planned 2026-11-26 (the user: "the way power works in Logicim is a bit weird / broken.  Power is instantaneously
applied on connection", then "a proper power-up model.  Let's go on a tangent and plan it").  Nothing is built.  The
electronics choices here are mine (the user, 2026-11-26: "whatever you suggest goes"); the questions at the end are
the user's.  Beside LogiCim's KicadImporterLib/ResistorsPlan.txt and PullsPlan.txt, which this leans on for the
weak drive (step 7).


How power works today
---------------------

  - A supply (CPowerSource, its CPowerOutPort) drives its fixed voltage from the moment it is connected, and always.
    Nothing switches it on or off, and nothing ramps it.
  - A chip asks IsPowered when it acts (an input transition, a tick): VCC above zero and GND zero.  Any VCC counts:
    0.1V powers a chip (CFamilyVoltageConfiguration::HasPower is VCC not zero).
  - A chip starts once, when the simulation starts (CIntegratedCircuit::Reset: ResetState, SimulationStarted,
    WriteOutputs), powered or not.  A chip without power slews forever.
  - Nothing tells a chip its power came or went: CIntegratedCircuit::TraceConnected does nothing, and a chip whose
    supply is cut keeps driving what it last drove.  A chip wired to a supply while the simulation runs never starts
    (its outputs stay as an unpowered chip left them) until something it reads changes.


What the HCP65 does at power-up (HCP65/Kicad/Computer, HCP65/Datasheets)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - Power and Reset: 18V in (J1), the power switch S1, D1, an L7812 to 12V, LD1117s to 5V and 3.3V, 100uF on every
    rail.  The other boards make their own 5V from 12V (23 LD1117S50Cs; dropout about 1.1V).  The Processor Reset
    Device makes its 3.3V from 5V (AP7365: start-up 200us).
  - Reset: a DS1813-10 on 5V holds ~{RST} low until VCC passes 4.35V (4.25 to 4.49), then for 150ms (100 to 300)
    more; pulled low (the reset button, or the Processor Reset Device's 74LVC1G125, a software reset) it holds reset
    150ms after the pull ends.  Its output is open drain with a 5.5k internal pull-up.  Then a 74LVC14, 20 ohm and
    10pF, another 74LVC14: ~{Reset}.
  - MAX6816 switch debouncers (Clock Push Button, the Mainboards): 50ms (20 to 80), undervoltage lockout 1.9 to 2.6V.

What a simulation of power-up should show: the rails rise, each chip comes alive as its rail passes its working
voltage, its outputs rise with the rail, and nothing is trusted until reset is released, 150ms after 5V is good.


The model (decided)
-------------------

  - A supply has a switch and a rise time: switched on, its voltage rises in a straight line from 0 to its voltage
    over its rise time (a regulator charging its output capacitor at its current limit); switched off, it falls the
    same way.  An imported board's rise time is 1ms (100uF at a regulator's current limit), a named constant.
  - A supply can be fed by another (a regulator): it switches on when its feed passes its voltage plus its dropout
    (LD1117: 1.1V), and off when its feed falls below that.
  - The rise is a series of steps (every 0.1V), each an ordinary change of the supply's trace, so the machinery that
    exists sees each one.  At every step each chip on the rail re-reads its inputs and re-drives its outputs at the
    new VCC (the family's voltages at that VCC: CFamilyVoltageConfiguration already interpolates).
  - A chip works at or above its family's lowest VCC in PropagationTimes.csv (LVC 1.8V), GND at 0, not at any VCC
    above zero.  Below it the chip is off: it drives nothing and ignores its inputs.
  - Power coming, by any way (a supply switched on or rising past the working voltage, a wire to VCC or GND
    connected while running), starts a chip: its state at power-up (below), SimulationStarted, WriteOutputs.
    Power going stops it: its outputs undriven (their traces' other ports told, CTrace::NotifyUndriven), its events
    off the timeline, its state forgotten.
  - What a chip remembers when its power comes (a flip-flop's bit, a counter, a register) is a setting of the
    simulation, the power-up state (the user, 2026-11-26):
      Random        each bit high or low from a seed the simulation keeps: as a real part comes up, the same seed
                    giving the same run, another seed showing whether reset sets everything.  LogiCim's default.
      Zero          every bit low, as ResetState gives now.  For tests, and for logic without power-up noise.
      Half supply   each output a chip drives from what it remembers at VCC/2 until the chip is reset or clocked:
                    neither high nor low, so whatever reset doesn't set shows.  An input reads VCC/2 as unsettled,
                    which is ignored, so a flip-flop fed back to itself never starts in this mode.
  - Not modelled: a chip powered through its inputs' clamp diodes, inrush current, a rail sagging under load, brown
    out glitches.


Steps
-----

MicroprocessorLib (with logic.md's sections on voltages, traces and chips, and its decisions)

1. CPowerSource: on and off (and times to switch, for tests and saved simulations), its rise time, its feed and
   dropout.  CPowerOutPort's voltage at a time from its ramp; each 0.1V step a change on its trace.
2. A chip's working voltage: CFamilyVoltageConfiguration's lowest VCC (CFixedVoltageConfiguration: any VCC above
   zero, as now); IsPowered uses it.
3. Power events.  A chip's CPowerInPorts tell it when its power comes, goes or steps (from the supply's steps,
   TraceConnected, Disconnect, NotifyUndriven on the rail): a PowerChanged on CIntegratedCircuit that starts, stops
   or re-drives it as the model says.  Gates re-evaluate their inputs; chips that remember re-drive through
   WriteOutputs.
4. Start at power-up.  CIntegratedCircuit::Reset at the simulation's start starts only powered chips; the others
   start when their power comes.
5. The power-up state.  CCircuitSimulation keeps the setting and the seed (Zero unless set, so the tests and every
   caller keep what they do now) and a random number generator from the seed, drawn in a fixed order (the chips'
   order in the circuit) so a run repeats.  ResetState is given the state: Zero as now, Random its bits from the
   generator, Half supply a mark that WriteOutputs drives VCC/2 (the family's VCC at that time halved) until the
   chip's next reset or clock sets it.  The seed and setting saved with a simulation's state.
6. Tests: the power-up state: Zero as now; Random the same twice from one seed and different from another; Half
   supply driving VCC/2, read as unsettled by an input, until a reset or clock.  A chip starting when its rail's ramp passes the working voltage, at the time worked out by hand; its high
   output rising with the rail; power cut, its outputs undriven and its traces' readers told; a VCC wire connected
   while running starting a chip; a regulator fed by a ramping rail switching on after its dropout.

Supervisors (after LogiCim's PullsPlan.txt: the DS1813's output is a pull)

7. A reset supervisor chip (the DS1813-10's figures; its trip voltage and reset time properties): ~{RST} low while
   VCC is under the trip voltage, and for the reset time after it passes it; pulled low from outside, low for the
   reset time after the pull ends; its output open drain with its 5.5k internal pull-up (PullsPlan's weak drive).
   The reset time is the datasheet's 150ms by default; a simulation may set it shorter, since running 150ms of the
   HCP65's clocks is millions of edges.
8. Tests: reset released the reset time after VCC passes the trip voltage; a 1us pushbutton pulse holding reset for
   the reset time; VCC dipping under the trip voltage asserting reset at once.

LogicimLib

9. A supply's view gets a switch, clicked to turn it on and off while running, and its rise time and feed in its
   properties; a ramping rail coloured by its voltage as any trace is.  The supervisor drawn as a chip with its pins.
   Saved and loaded in a circuit's XML and a simulation's state, as every other component is.
   The power-up state is a project's setting (Random, Zero or Half supply, and the seed), chosen beside the
   simulation's run controls and saved in the circuits file, not the user's Settings.xml, so a project runs the
   same for whoever opens it.  New projects are Random; a circuits file saved before this has none and loads as
   Zero, as it ran then.

KicadImporterLib

10. Supplies get the imported rise time.  A regulator (today the implied supply of the chips its output reaches)
   becomes a supply fed by its input's net, with its datasheet's dropout.  A DS1813 becomes a supervisor, found by a
   list of part numbers as ResistorsPlan's comparators are.
11. Tests: Power and Reset imported and switched on: 12V, then 5V and 3.3V rising, ~{Reset}_{5V} going high the
    reset time after 5V passes 4.35V, plus the 74LVC1G17's delay.  The Processor Reset Device: ~{Reset} high after
    the DS1813, the two 74LVC14s and the 20 ohm and 10pF; a software reset (IC8 driving ~{Reset Switch} low) giving a
    reset 150ms long.  The expected circuits regenerated (supplies' new properties only); TestMicroprocessorLib,
    TestLogicimLib, TestKicadImporterLib; the website's decode check.


Questions for the user (put one at a time, recommendation first)
------------------------------------------------------------------

  1. What a chip remembers when its power comes: decided 2026-11-26 (the user: "Put it in the PowerPlan.txt
     please"), a setting, the power-up state (the model, step 5, step 9), Random by default in LogiCim.
  2. A supply placed by hand, and every circuit saved before this: recommended on from time 0 with no rise, as now,
     so the tests and saved circuits keep their timing; imported boards rise over 1ms.  Or every supply rises over
     1ms, the timing of every test moving by it.
     Decided 2026-11-26 (the user): as recommended.
  3. Found while building (2026-11-26): LogiCim runs 0.25 simulated ns a real ms by default, so a 1ms ramp is 67
     minutes to watch and the DS1813's 150ms a week.  Decided (the user: "Fast-forward"): LogiCim runs flat out,
     without pacing, until every supply has arrived and every supervisor has let go, then at its speed; the timings
     stay real, and the reset time can still be shortened per simulation (step 7).
  4. Found while building (2026-11-26): the DS1813 needs PullsPlan.txt's weak drive, not built.  Decided (the user:
     "Build the weak drive now"): PullsPlan's simulation part first (a released line going to its pull level at
     once until ResistorsPlan's RC curve exists), then the DS1813 on it; the importer's pulls and their drawing later.
  5. Found while building (2026-11-26): a click on a component in LogiCim selects or moves it.  Decided (the user:
     "Power command"): Simulation, Power On/Off, with a key, switches the selected supplies, or every supply when none
     is selected (the board's power switch), in place of clicking a supply.
  6. Found while building (2026-11-26): an imported chip's power pins are hidden and each chip gets two private
     supplies at its implied VCC (PowerPortsImplicitly), so there is no rail for a regulator to drive.  Decided (the
     user: "Hidden power pins join their named rail"): a chip's hidden VCC and GND join the nets their KiCad power
     pins are on, by name, as tunnels of that name would; each rail gets one supply, a regulator a fed supply on its
     output rail.  The drawing doesn't change.
