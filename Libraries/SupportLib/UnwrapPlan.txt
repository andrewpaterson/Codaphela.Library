Texture unwrapping plan
=======================

Giving a mesh that has no UVs (an isosurface, a procedural mesh) a UV layer, so a texture can be drawn on it, and baking a
texture for it.  Nothing here is built yet.  What is decided and what is only proposed is said at each point.


The user's decisions (2026-11-15)
---------------------------------
  It lives in SupportLib: unwrapping is for any mesh, not only isosurfaces.
  Textures will be both baked and loaded; baked first.
  Texel density isn't known yet, so it's a setting, not fixed (see Texel density).


What there is
-------------
  CMesh's UVs are CMeshUVCoords: layers (CMeshUVLayer), each its UVs (CArrayLargeFloat32Vec2) and for each face its corners'
  UVs (SMeshUVFace, aiUVs[0] == -1 for a face with none).  Faces of different charts can share a corner position and still
  have different UVs, so a seam needs no extra corners.
  A material (CMaterial) has its images (CMaterialImage: a Ptr<CImage>, addressing, filters and meUVSource), and CMeshMaterials
  a material for each face.  RenderLib's cooked mesh carries UVs and RasterLib draws textured faces (TestRasterTexture).
  Modifiers that rebuild a mesh's faces (weld, merge normals, CMeshShapeNormals) refuse a mesh with UVs (CanRebuild), so an
  unwrap goes on an editor's stack after them.
  There's no sparse linear solver, no procedural colour (noise, a material of 3D points) and no bake.


How it's done
-------------
Three stages, as every unwrapper does them (Hormann, Levy and Sheffer's course notes; xatlas):

  Charts     The mesh is cut into charts, pieces that flatten without much stretch.  Their edges are the seams.
  Flatten    Each chart is laid flat (parameterised) so its triangles keep their shapes as nearly as can be.
  Pack       The charts are placed in the unit square without overlapping, a few texels apart, at one scale for all.

Then for a baked texture:

  Bake       For each texel inside a chart, its triangle and barycentric position give a 3D point and normal on the mesh, and
             the material's colour there is the texel's.  Texels just outside each chart take the colours of their nearest
             inside ones (dilation), so filtering and mip levels don't bring in the gutter's colour at seams.

Baking is why seams are cheap here: every chart is baked from its own 3D points, so neighbouring charts match at a seam without
their UVs having to line up, as a painted texture's must.  Short seams matter less than little stretch.  A loaded texture,
later, is the other way round: it comes with its UVs, or wants few, long-lined-up seams.


Charts (proposed)
-----------------
First, box projection: each face goes to whichever of the six axis directions its normal is nearest, and each connected group
of faces with one direction is a chart, projected flat along it.  It can't fold, and gets the whole pipeline drawing.  It
stretches on slopes (up to 1 / cos 54.7 degrees, a face at the same angle to three axes) and makes many seams on a curve.

Then, charts grown by normals (as xatlas and Blender's Smart UV Project do, D-Charts done properly): faces are added to a chart
while their normals stay within an angle of its average, best fitting face first, then charts too small are merged into a
neighbour.  A chart has to be a disc (one boundary, no holes) to flatten: one that isn't is split.

Proposed: grown charts, angle 60 degrees, as LSCM below flattens a curved chart well.


Flatten (proposed)
------------------
Least Squares Conformal Maps (Levy, Petitjean, Ray and Maillot 2002): the UVs minimising how far each triangle is from a
scaled, turned copy of itself, with two corners pinned (the furthest apart on the chart's boundary).  It's a sparse linear least
squares problem in the UVs, solved by conjugate gradients on its normal equations.  Angles are kept, so a texture isn't
sheared; areas can grow or shrink across a big chart, which charting by angle keeps small.

A chart LSCM folds (a triangle turned over in UV) is split in two and each flattened again.

Later, if texel density across a chart matters: As-Rigid-As-Possible (Liu, Zhang, Xu, Gotsman and Gortler 2008) from LSCM's
UVs, which keeps areas too.

The solver: a sparse matrix (rows of column, value pairs) and conjugate gradients.  Proposed in BaseLib, as plain arithmetic,
float64 inside (a big chart's normal equations lose float32 precision).


Pack (proposed)
---------------
First, each chart's bounding rectangle, turned to its smallest area, packed by size into the square (skyline), all charts at
one scale, a few texels between them.  Then, where space is wasted, charts drawn into a bitmap at a few turns and placed where
they first fit (xatlas's way).

Texel density
-------------
Not known yet.  Proposed: a setting, texels per unit of the mesh, from which the packer works out the image size (rounded up to
a power of two); or an image size, from which it works out the density.  Every chart has the same density, so a texture's
detail is the same everywhere.


Bake (proposed)
---------------
A material of 3D points: a CObject whose Colour(position, normal) gives an ARGB32, so a bake doesn't care what makes the
colour.  The first is a 3D checker, which is also the test of stretch: squares that stay square and the same size everywhere
mean the unwrap is good.  Noise and real materials are another plan.

The baker draws each face's UV triangle into a CImage at the packed size, with each texel's 3D point and normal from its
barycentric position, asks the material, then dilates the charts' edges into the gutters.  The image becomes the mesh's
material's diffuse image.


Steps
-----
Each with its tests, in TestSupportLib.

1. Measures, as TestProceduralLib's MeshMeasures are for meshes: every face has UVs, none is turned over, no two charts'
   triangles overlap, all are in the unit square, and how much each triangle is stretched (the singular values of its map from
   3D to UV: their ratio is angle distortion, their product against the chart's average is area distortion).
2. Box projection and bounding box packing, as a CMeshModifier (CMeshUnwrap) that adds a UV layer.  Tests: a box (six charts,
   no stretch), a sphere (stretched as box projection is), a torus, an isosurface; all measures pass.
3. Bake: the 3D material, the checker, the baker and dilation, and the image on the mesh's material.  Tests: texels' colours
   against the checker at their 3D points, the gutters filled, and a scene drawn by the raster renderer within a tolerance of
   saved bytes, as TestShapeScene.
4. Grown charts.  Tests: a sphere in few charts, a box in six, every chart a disc.
5. BaseLib's sparse matrix and conjugate gradients.  Tests: against small systems solved directly.
6. LSCM.  Tests: a flat chart's UVs its shape exactly (no stretch), a hemisphere's angles kept, folded charts split; the
   checker's squares stay square on the sphere and isosurface.
7. Raster packing, if bounding boxes waste much space.
8. ARAP, if texel density across charts matters.
9. Loaded textures: keeping a mesh's own UVs, and unwrapping for a texture made to be painted (fewer, longer seams).


Open
----
Texel density (above).
How the checker or a material is given its scale.
Mip levels: whether a baked image makes its own, and how wide the gutters must be for them.
Several UV layers: one for colour, and later a lightmap's (which can't share texels between faces, unlike a tiled colour).
Whether CMeshUnwrap keeps its charts so a re-bake after a material change doesn't unwrap again.
