Texture unwrapping plan
=======================

Giving a mesh that has no UVs (an isosurface, a procedural mesh) a UV layer, so it can have a texture of its own, and baking that
texture.  The aim is a single megatexture, as Rage's: every surface its own texels, nothing tiled (VirtualTexturePlan.txt).
Steps 1 to 6 are built (see Steps).  What is decided and what is only proposed is said at each point.


The user's decisions (2026-11-15)
---------------------------------
  It lives in SupportLib: unwrapping is for any mesh, not only isosurfaces.
  Textures will be both baked and loaded; baked first.
  The texturing is to be a megatexture, as Rage's, on the object database (VirtualTexturePlan.txt).  The user then took these
  recommendations for it:
    Texel density is one setting for the whole scene, texels per world unit, the same on every surface; its value is chosen
    by eye once something is textured.  Every chart is laid out at its true size in texels, not scaled to fill an image.
    Every mip level is baked from the materials, not shrunk from the level above, and each level's gutters are dilated.
    Charts are packed onto the virtual texture's page grid where they can be, so a chart's pages are mostly its own; gutters
    are about 4 texels at the finest level.
    One UV layer, unique texels.  Lighting and decals bake into the same texels, so there is no lightmap layer.  Loaded
    textures that tile are a second layer, for those materials only, later.
    Where a mesh's charts are is kept, so a change of material or light re-bakes the pages it touches without unwrapping
    again.
    Materials give a colour at a 3D point and are scaled in world units, so texel density changes how sharp they are, never
    how they look.
    An instance that bakes lighting has texels of its own; small repeated props can share their mesh's.
    The UVs are made ready for the virtual texture now, but the first textures are ordinary images, one for each mesh, drawn
    by the renderers as they are; the virtual texture's paging comes after, in its own plan.


What there is
-------------
  CMesh's UVs are CMeshUVCoords: layers (CMeshUVLayer), each its UVs (CArrayLargeFloat32Vec2) and for each face its corners'
  UVs (SMeshUVFace, aiUVs[0] == -1 for a face with none).  Faces of different charts can share a corner position and still
  have different UVs, so a seam needs no extra corners.
  A material (CMaterial) has its images (CMaterialImage: a Ptr<CImage>, addressing, filters and meUVSource), and CMeshMaterials
  a material for each face.  RenderLib's cooked mesh carries UVs and RasterLib draws textured faces (TestRasterTexture).
  Modifiers that rebuild a mesh's faces (weld, merge normals, CMeshShapeNormals) refuse a mesh with UVs (CanRebuild), so an
  unwrap goes on an editor's stack after them.
  There's no sparse linear solver, no procedural colour (noise, a material of 3D points) and no bake.


How it's done
-------------
Three stages, as every unwrapper does them (Hormann, Levy and Sheffer's course notes; xatlas):

  Charts     The mesh is cut into charts, pieces that flatten without much stretch.  Their edges are the seams.
  Flatten    Each chart is laid flat (parameterised) so its triangles keep their shapes as nearly as can be.
  Pack       The charts are placed in a rectangle of texels without overlapping, a gutter apart, at the scene's texel
             density: the mesh's layout.

Then:

  Bake       For each texel inside a chart, its triangle and barycentric position give a 3D point and normal on the mesh, and
             the material's colour there is the texel's.  Texels in the gutters take the colours of their nearest charts'
             (dilation), so filtering doesn't bring in the gutter's colour at a seam.  Every mip level is baked this way.

Baking is why seams are cheap: every chart is baked from its own 3D points, so neighbouring charts match at a seam without their
UVs having to line up, as a painted texture's must.  Little stretch matters more than short seams.


The layout and where it goes
----------------------------
A mesh's layout is its charts packed into a rectangle, measured in texels at the scene's density, its sides whole pages.  The
mesh's UV layer is each corner's place in that rectangle (0 to 1 across it).  Instances share a mesh, so where a copy of the
layout is in the virtual texture belongs to the instance: its address, in pages.  Repeated props point at one copy; an instance
that bakes lighting has its own.  Until the virtual texture is built, the layout is an ordinary image the size of the rectangle,
the mesh's material's diffuse image.

An instance scaled up has fewer texels to the unit than the scene's density.  Proposed: allowed for now (an instance's scale is
usually near 1); an instance with its own copy could have a layout at its scale later.


Charts (proposed)
-----------------
First, box projection: each face goes to whichever of the six axis directions its normal is nearest, and each connected group of
faces with one direction is a chart, projected flat along it.  It can't fold, and gets the whole pipeline drawing.  It stretches
on slopes (up to 1 / cos 54.7 degrees, a face at the same angle to three axes) and makes many seams on a curve.

Then charts grown by normals (as xatlas and Blender's Smart UV Project do, D-Charts done properly): faces are added to a chart
while their normals stay within an angle of its average, best fitting face first, then charts too small are merged into a
neighbour.  A chart has to be a disc (one boundary, no holes) to flatten: one that isn't is split.  Proposed angle: 60 degrees,
as LSCM below flattens a curved chart well.


Flatten (proposed)
------------------
Least Squares Conformal Maps (Levy, Petitjean, Ray and Maillot 2002): the UVs minimising how far each triangle is from a scaled,
turned copy of itself, with two corners pinned (the furthest apart on the chart's boundary).  It's a sparse linear least
squares problem in the UVs, solved by conjugate gradients on its normal equations.  Angles are kept, so a texture isn't
sheared.  A chart's scale is then set so its area in texels is its area in the mesh at the scene's density.

A chart LSCM folds (a triangle turned over in UV) is split in two and each flattened again.

Areas can still grow or shrink across a big chart, so texel density isn't quite even inside it.  If that shows, As-Rigid-As-
Possible (Liu, Zhang, Xu, Gotsman and Gortler 2008) from LSCM's UVs keeps areas too.

The solver: a sparse matrix (rows of column, value pairs) and conjugate gradients, in BaseLib as plain arithmetic, float64
inside (a big chart's normal equations lose float32 precision).


Pack (proposed)
---------------
Each chart's bounding rectangle, turned to its smallest area, packed by size (skyline) into the layout, a gutter apart, its
corner on a page boundary where that wastes little.  Later, where space is wasted, charts drawn into a bitmap at a few turns
and placed where they first fit (xatlas's way).  The layout's sides are rounded up to whole pages.


Bake (proposed)
---------------
A material of 3D points: a CObject whose Colour(position, normal) gives an ARGB32, its scale in world units, so a bake doesn't
care what makes the colour.  The first is a 3D checker, which is also the test of stretch: squares that stay square and the
same size everywhere mean the unwrap is good.  Noise and real materials are another plan.

The baker draws each face's UV triangle into the layout's image at each mip level, each texel's 3D point and normal from its
barycentric position, asks the material, then dilates the charts' edges into the gutters.


Steps
-----
Each with its tests, in TestSupportLib (those of isosurfaces in TestProceduralLib, which has them).

1. Measures, as TestProceduralLib's MeshMeasures are for meshes: every face has UVs, none is turned over, no two charts'
   triangles overlap, all are in the layout, and how much each triangle is stretched (the singular values of its map from 3D
   to texels: their ratio is angle distortion, their product against the scene's density squared is area distortion).
   Done: MeshUVMeasures.  A face anticlockwise seen from outside is to be anticlockwise in UV, u right and v up; one that
   isn't, or has no area in UV, is turned over.  Overlaps are pairs of faces whose UV triangles' insides share area (the
   separating axis test, touching not counted), found through a grid of cells over the UVs.
2. Box projection and bounding box packing at a texel density, as a CMeshModifier (CMeshUnwrap) that adds the
   UV layer and keeps the layout's size and its charts.  Tests: a box (six charts, no stretch), a sphere (stretched as box
   projection is), a torus, an isosurface; all measures pass.
   Done: CMeshUnwrap.  Charts are joined across edges between faces of one direction; a corner on a seam has a UV for each
   chart it's in.  Packing is by bounding rectangles along a skyline, tallest first, the layout as wide as the square root of
   the charts' area, not yet onto pages (that waits for the virtual texture).  It keeps the layout's size and number of charts;
   each chart's rectangle, which needs an array saved with it, also waits for the virtual texture.  A box is six charts with
   no stretch; a sphere six, within root 3; an isosurface of five spheres unwrapped after CMeshShapeNormals, all clean.
3. Bake: the 3D material, the checker, the baker, every mip level, dilation, and the image on the mesh's material.  Tests:
   texels' colours against the checker at their 3D points at every level, the gutters filled, and a scene drawn by the raster
   renderer within a tolerance of saved bytes, as TestShapeScene.
   Done: CSolidTexture (Colour(position, normal, width): the texel's width in world units, so a texture finer than a texel
   gives its average, not its noise), CSolidChecker (Quilez's box filtered checker in three dimensions, a point's colour below
   a thousandth of a cube, where float32 can't average), BakeMeshTexture (any UV layer at any size: texels whose centres a face
   covers take the texture at their 3D points, then every other texel its nearest covered one's, breadth first) and CMeshBake
   (level 0 at the layout's size, read from the CMeshUnwrap before it, on one white material).  Only level 0 is baked, as
   neither renderer has mipmaps; BakeMeshTexture at half the size is the next level, for the virtual texture.  A face lying on
   a checker's cube planes is evenly both colours, as a solid texture is there.  TestShapeScene's textured scene: a box and
   five spheres smoothly joined, checkered, within a tolerance of the frame first looked at.
4. Grown charts.  Tests: a sphere in few charts, a box in six, every chart a disc.
   Done: CMeshCharts (MakeBox, Grow, Regrow) and CMeshUnwrap's meCharts (MUC_Box still the default, until LSCM).  A chart
   grows across edges from each face not yet in one, taking whichever face beside it is nearest its area weighted average
   normal while that's within 60 degrees; faces that end up more than 85 degrees from the final average leave it, so a chart
   laid flat across its normal can't fold.  There's no disc check: a chart only has to lay flat without overlapping itself
   until LSCM, and one that overlaps (found by the measures' overlap test) has its faces grown again at half the angle.  Charts
   too small aren't merged yet, and every chart is turned (by 90 turns of a degree) to its smallest bounding rectangle.  A box
   is six charts unstretched; a sphere six, up to 4.6 times stretched where its faces slope from the chart's normal (a mean of
   1.37); a torus 23; an isosurface of five spheres clean in both kinds.  Turning box charts moved the textured scene's
   texels, so its expected frame is the new one.
5. BaseLib's sparse matrix and conjugate gradients.  Tests: against small systems solved directly.
   Done: CFloat64SparseMatrix (entries as row, column and value in any order, two at one place adding; A x and A' x),
   Float64SparseSolve (conjugate gradients, A symmetric positive definite) and Float64SparseLeastSquares (CGLS: the normal
   equations without making A'A, so LSCM's matrix is built as it is, a row for each triangle's two equations).  Both stop at a
   residual relative to x = 0's, return their iterations or -1.  TestFloat64SparseMatrix: multiplies against a dense matrix,
   a 3 by 3 system in three iterations, a 100 long one, a line fitted to five points (two iterations), 300 equations in 60
   unknowns, and a matrix that isn't positive definite failing.
6. LSCM.  Tests: a flat chart's UVs its shape exactly (no stretch), a hemisphere's angles kept, folded charts split; the checker's
   squares stay square on the sphere and isosurface.
   Done: FlattenConformal (MeshUVConformal) and CMeshUnwrap's meFlatten (MUF_Projected, the default for now, or
   MUF_Conformal).  A chart's rows are each triangle's Cauchy-Riemann equations in its own plane, weighted by the root of its
   area; the two UVs furthest apart laid across the normal are pinned, and that layout is CGLS's start; the answer is scaled
   to the chart's area at the density.  A chart that folds or overlaps itself goes back through step 4's regrow (split by
   growing again at half the angle), so folds are split as overlaps are; neither has happened on the test meshes.  Grown
   charts by LSCM: a sphere's faces' angles kept to 1.11 times (4.6 laid across their normals), a torus's 1.09, the five
   sphere isosurface's 1.10 (7.4), the hemisphere's 1.23 with its areas 0.51 to 2.04 of the density, as a conformal map's
   are.  Conjugate gradients make about as many iterations as a chart has UVs (column scaling saved 13%, not kept), so
   TestMeshUnwrap is too slow for the W65C816's 4G instructions (47.8G cycles) and isn't run there.  CountUVTrianglesTurned
   and MeasureUVTriangleStretch measure a chart's own triangles.  A baked solid texture looks the same however it's
   unwrapped (only its sharpness changes), so the textured scene doesn't show it; the measures do.
7. Raster packing, if bounding boxes waste much space.
8. ARAP, if texel density inside charts matters.
9. The virtual texture (VirtualTexturePlan.txt): layouts placed at instances' addresses, pages baked on demand.
10. Loaded textures: a mesh's own UVs kept, tiling materials on a second layer.


Open
----
The scene's texel density, chosen by eye (step 3 makes it easy to try).
How large a chart may be before it's split for packing (a chart wider than the virtual texture's page cache can't be drawn
at its finest level all at once).
