Scene plan
==========

CScene, CInstance, CConnection, CConnectionAnimation, CCamera and CLight describe a scene, in floats.  They aren't used yet,
and CScene still rests on CFakeTracker and TrackerTypes.  A renderer draws what is converted from them, one way, from the
description to what is drawn.  RasterLib is the renderer today and a Vulkan one is to come, so nothing here is only for
RasterLib unless it says so.


What the objects are
--------------------
  CScene                    Instances, lights and cameras.  Everything else is reached through what uses it.
    CInstance               One thing in the game, one character say.
      CArray<CConnection>   Its own, in slot order.
      CArray<CInstanceMesh>
        Ptr<CMeshEditor>    Shared with other instances.  Holds the mesh and its modifier stack.
        Connection slots    What each of the mesh's matrices uses.  One for a rigid mesh, one per matrix for a skinned one.
        Materials           In place of the mesh's defaults.
    CLight, CCamera         Not instanced.  Each has its own Ptr<CConnection>.

Two characters are two CInstances that share their meshes and bones and are drawn in different places.

A CConnection is something spatial that lights, cameras, meshes or other game information can be attached to.

  Kind        What                                 Reaches its connection by
  Shared      CMeshEditor, bones                   The instance holds it, and slots in the instance's connections
  Not shared  CLight, CCamera, small game objects  Its own Ptr<CConnection>

Shared data can't point at a connection, as each instance has its own.  It refers to one by slot, so the order of an
instance's connections matters and is saved with it.  A skinned mesh's SSkinWeight::iMatrix numbers its own matrices, not the
instance's connections, which is why the slots are per mesh.

Lights and cameras are too small to be worth sharing and aren't instanced.  Two lanterns are two CLights.  A light on its own
has its own connection, and one carried by a character has a connection whose parent is the character's hand.

Pose (skinning, morphs) is per instance and never changes the shared CMesh, as two characters share it and stand differently.

CMeshEditor holds the mesh and the modifier stack and produces the final mesh to be cooked.


From the scene to what is drawn
-------------------------------
  CMeshEditor::GetOutput --> CMesh                                  Runs the stack.
    1. Cook: when it has changed.                                   SRenderMeshData and friends.  Plain, for any renderer.
    2. Create: per device.                                          CRasterRenderMesh or CVulkanRenderMesh, from CRenderMesh.
    3. Extract: every frame, from connections, camera and lights.   SRenderFrame
    4. Draw.                                                        CRasteriser, or Vulkan command buffers.

  Source                 Cooked or extracted             Raster                     Vulkan
  CMesh                  Unique vertices, submeshes by   CRasterMesh                Vertex and index buffers, ranges
                         material slot, opaque and
                         transparent, bounds
  CMaterial              Parameters, image references    Flat colour at most        Descriptor set, pipeline key
  CImage                 Pixels in a format, with mips   Ignored for now            VkImage, view, sampler
  CMeshSkin              Weights, inverse matrices       Skinned on the CPU         Vertex attributes, matrix palette
  CConnection            World matrix, parity            asAxes and sOffset         Per instance buffer, front face
  CCamera                View and projection             CRasterCamera              Uniform buffer
  CLight                 A list of lights                CRasteriser's light values Uniform buffer
  CConnectionAnimation   (changes CConnection)           Never sees it              Never sees it

A cooked mesh keeps a table from the CMesh's corners to its vertices, while it is being edited, for updating it in place.
Position and normal are a stream of their own, so that a change of shape or pose only touches them.  CRasterMesh already
splits this way, with masVertices apart from its triangles.

The integer scale, quantising and turn-only axes are the raster renderer's own, and so is what it does with a mirrored matrix.


When something changes
----------------------
Each frame CRenderScene walks the scene and looks at the editors' change counters.  Nothing has to notify it.

  Change                                   What happens
  Structure, or any edit under modifiers   The stack runs again and a new render mesh replaces the old at a frame boundary.
  Shape, with no modifiers                 The same at first.  Updated in place once something needs that speed.
  Pose                                     Per instance.  The shared CMesh and the stack aren't touched.
  A mesh no longer used                    Its Ptr is dropped and the graph frees it.

Modifiers may add or remove vertices, so a mesh with any modifiers can't be updated in place: there may be no old vertex to
overwrite.  A heavy stack is too slow to run on every mouse move, so an editor may run it when a drag ends, or draw the
unmodified mesh during the drag.  That is the editor's choice.

The graph can free a render mesh at any moment, while the GPU may still be using it.  On Vulkan its Free only queues its buffers
until that frame's fence signals.  Raster frees them at once.

Destruction order is what CComponent does with Nes(M_Pointer(mpWindow)).  A render mesh holds a Nes pointer to its CRenderer, so
every mesh is freed before the renderer, and the pointer is still valid in the mesh's Free.  The renderer's lookup pointing back
at its meshes is fine: only a cycle made entirely of Nes pointers can't be ordered (see TestObjectFreeOrdering).  The renderer's
Free waits for the device, empties the queue and destroys the device.  The factory isn't an object in the graph and is made by the
app, so it outlives them all, as CNativeWindowFactory does CWindow.  A mesh's Free shouldn't read a collection on the renderer:
after an explicit Kill only pointer fields are put back for it, not collection entries.


The rule
--------
Nothing in the scene knows a renderer exists, and a renderer only sees what it is given.

  SupportLib        The scene.  Knows nothing of renderers.
  RenderLib         Cooking, CRenderer (per device, holds the shared resources), CRenderScene, the abstract CRenderMesh and
                    friends, and the abstract CRenderFactory.  The app makes the factory, as it does CNativeWindowFactory.
  RasterRenderLib   Implements the factory using RasterLib.  Converts floats and scene types into what RasterLib wants.
  VulkanLib         Implements the factory using Vulkan.
  RasterLib         A standalone fixed point renderer.  Takes integers and draws, and is usable with no scene at all.  Standalone
                    means no SupportLib or RenderLib.  BaseLib and StandardLib are part of it.

These stay out of RasterLib:
  Transforms with parents        CConnection's local and world matrices.
  Lights as objects              CLight.  The rasteriser is given plain values to shade with.
  Keyframes or time              CConnectionAnimation.
  A list of what's in the world  CScene.

Drawing features in fixed point go in RasterLib, and whatever touches floats or scene types goes in RasterRenderLib, which is a
library of its own so that RasterLib depends on neither RenderLib nor the scene.  Today RasterLib isn't quite standalone:
CRasterMesh::Build includes SupportLib/Mesh.h, until step 4.  CRasterCamera keeps its rotation in a float matrix, which it
converts to fixed point once a frame.

A CScene never creates a CRasterMesh.  RasterRenderLib does.

CRasterMesh is a CObject, so that anything can share one through a Ptr, with or without a scene.  SRasterInstance stays a plain
struct with a raw pointer, as it is per-frame data used by the worker threads.  Whatever builds the list of instances holds the
Ptr<CRasterMesh>s, which keeps them alive during the draw.

Each renderer subclasses CRenderMesh directly.  CCanvas has a native object beneath it because it has a lot of logic that doesn't
depend on the host.  A render mesh has little, so that layer would buy almost nothing, and a native object couldn't hold a Ptr.


Steps
-----
Each is done when something needs it, with tests.  Skinning must be proved before Vulkan.

1. Scene objects.  Remove CFakeTracker and TrackerTypes.  CScene becomes a CObject with its three lists.  Write CInstance and
   CInstanceMesh.  CLight and CCamera each get a Ptr<CConnection>.  CMeshMaterials' mcMaterials and mcFaces and
   CMaterialImage::miImageID are indices into trackers: faces hold slots (-1 for no material), the mesh's list becomes Ptrs, and
   CMaterial holds the Ptr<CImage>s, leaving CMaterialImage with how they are sampled.
2. CMeshEditor.  CMeshEditor::Init gives its stack the mesh, as it has no source today, and GetOutput returns the stack's.  With
   no modifiers that is the mesh itself.  Structure and shape counters go on the editor's methods.  The renderer clears the
   output after cooking.
   Polygons and edge visibility move to CMesh as opt-in details, as they describe the shape and an output mesh needs them.  Being
   opt-in (mbInUse) keeps big meshes from generating connectivity for every face they add.  CMesh copies them, so macBaseEdges goes
   from CMeshEditor, and CMeshSmoothGroups can smooth by angle in Modify.  A modifier that knows about polygons rebuilds them.  Any
   other that changes faces loses them, and its output is plain triangles.  CMeshEditor's AddFace still takes edge flags and writes
   them into the mesh.
   Selections stay on the editor.  They are what the user is doing, not the shape, and they describe the mesh being edited.
   Apply and CMeshEditor::Touch then go, leaving only Modify and GetOutput.  Every modifier with an Apply also has a Modify.
   CMeshEditor::Collapse uses Touch, so it changes too.  GetOutput doesn't wait for any of this.
3. CConnection gets a Ptr<CConnection> parent and updates: a world matrix from a local one and the parent's.
4. RenderLib and RasterRenderLib: create and replace only.  CRasterMesh::Build moves to RasterRenderLib, the same code, so that
   RasterLib no longer includes SupportLib/Mesh.h.  BoardScene, VRMLCanvasDraw and any tests that build from a CMesh call it
   there.  Check whether SupportLib/ColourARGB32.h can go with it.  CRasterMesh becomes a CObject: BoardScene and VRMLCanvasDraw
   hold it by value through U_Data and TestRasterLib has many as locals, so they change too.
5. Extract.
   Connections become SRasterInstances, one for each rigid mesh of each instance: axes and offset from the world matrix of the
   mesh's connection, in one scene-wide scale.  Decide what happens to a mirrored matrix (mbParity), as SRasterInstance must turn
   and not mirror, and to one that scales.
   CCamera and its connection set up a CRasterCamera.  ECameraType decides the projection, so ERasterProjection isn't set
   separately, and FOV becomes focal length.  Drag, Pan, Zoom and FitOrbit stay on CRasterCamera as viewer controls.  Decide how
   they write back to the scene, if they should.
   CRasteriser takes light values, in place of msLight and RASTER_AMBIENT, with what they are now as the default.
6. Move BoardScene onto a CScene: its models, the mainboard and boards standing in their sockets become instances and
   connections, and its yaw a connection or an animation.  Its pixels must stay byte-identical.  The VRML read becomes lazy, so
   that a cached model needs no CMesh.  This is what proves the rigid path.
7. CConnectionAnimation: keyframes change connections, and step 5 runs again.  miConnectionIndex is a slot in the instance's
   connections.  Sharing a walk also needs a clip that groups the tracks of many connections, as one CConnectionAnimation is one
   connection.
8. Skinning, in software on the raster renderer: per-instance vertices, using the slots from step 1.  The fixed point skinning
   goes in RasterLib, taking integer weights and bone matrices, and RasterRenderLib converts to them.  Goober proves it.  Needs
   modifiers that keep UVs and skin (see Open) and a conversion from COldDragonScene to CScene.
9. In-place updates, if dragging plain meshes is slow.
10. The cook stage and VulkanLib.  The factory, and the build in RasterRenderLib, change from CMesh* to cooked data.
    This is left until now so that BoardScene's vertex order and pixels aren't put at risk for nothing.


Test scenes
-----------
BoardScene proves the rigid path, including the parent chain.  It has no textures, materials or skinning.

Goober, a skinned mesh walking a field of stars, proves the rest: D:\Work\Star Control\Builds\ViewerV7beta2009.9.20\Goober.DRG
and Particles\Stars.csv beside it, read by OldDragonLib.  1 mesh (3241 positions, 6134 faces), 1 material, 3 images, 67 bones
and 1105 stars.  The Max source is in D:\Work\Bounty Arms\MaxFiles\Goober.

  Pose it with vectors on the left of row-major matrices:
    fWeight * (position * inverse[iMatrix] * keyframe world of maiConnections[iMatrix])
  Use key frames, not its connections' matrices, which tear the mesh.
  Its keys look to hold world matrices, so it may not exercise the parent chain.
  SOldDragonKeyFrame::fTime is seconds since the previous key.  CAnimKeyFrame::mfTime is commented as time until the next
  frame, so don't copy one to the other.
  The walk is on the spot.  Moving across the stars is the instance's own connection.
  It has no stored normals.  They come from its smoothing groups.

Melee.DRG has 64 meshes with no faces, but AddOldDragonMaxFaces with D:\Work\Star Control\MaxFiles\Melee.max restores their faces,
UVs, names and materials (132,356 triangles), and OldDragonLib reads their colours.  Placement comes from the DRG's connections
and instances, as the .max's node transforms weren't decoded when last reported.  A textured material's colours are white in a
.DRG but not in the .max.


Open
----
The scene file format.  A generic 3D scene format is to be designed once the old readers in OldDragonLib are finished.  It has to
decide what is stored: the CMeshEditor with its mesh and stack, the final mesh, or both.  That decides whether a loaded scene can
still be edited and whether a cached model loads without its CMesh.  The old files show what to avoid: layouts that changed
between versions, raw pointers stored in arrays, and saves that failed without anything noticing.

Particles.  Goober's stars are neither a mesh nor an instance.  Either CScene gets a fourth list or they are something else, and
a point list has its own cook and draw on each renderer.

Modifiers can't keep UVs, skin or materials.  CMeshModifier::CanRebuild refuses a mesh with any of them: it logs a warning and
Modify returns the mesh as it was, so the modifier does nothing.  Goober has them and needs its normals generated by a modifier,
so this must be fixed before step 8.

Where bone hierarchy lives.  CMeshSkin has the weights and inverse matrices, but nothing holds a skeleton that instances could
share.

Creating and removing an attached light.  Copying an instance doesn't bring its lantern along, so whatever creates the
character must also create the light and add it to the scene.  When the character is removed, something must remove the light,
or it stays lit where the hand was: its connection's parent keeps the hand's connection alive, but nothing updates it.

Coordinates.  Max's exports were Y-up and the KiCad boards look Z-up, so CMesh has no one convention.  Set one, and importers
convert to it.

Threads.  Cooking would run on CJobs and draws skip what isn't ready, but it isn't known yet that reading a CMesh off the main
thread is safe.

Vulkan in a window.  CCanvas is software pixels, while Vulkan presents to a surface made from the native window.  A Vulkan view in
a dialog needs a design of its own.
