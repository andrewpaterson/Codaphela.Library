Scene plan
==========

CScene, CInstance, CConnection, CConnectionAnimation, CCamera and CLight describe a scene, in floats.  They aren't used yet,
and CScene still rests on CFakeTracker and TrackerTypes.  RasterLib draws, in integers.  The split is the same as CMesh and
CRasterMesh: one way, from the description to what is drawn.


What the objects are
--------------------
A CInstance is one thing in the game, one character say.  It holds pointers to meshes and bone information, and has its own
connections.  Two characters are two CInstances that share their meshes and bones and are drawn in different places.

A CConnection is something spatial that lights, cameras, meshes or other game information can be attached to.

  Kind        What                                 Reaches its connection by
  Shared      CMesh, bones                         The instance holds it, and a slot in the instance's connections
  Not shared  CLight, CCamera, small game objects  Its own Ptr<CConnection>

Shared data can't point at a connection, as each instance has its own.  It refers to one by slot, so the order of an
instance's connections matters and is saved with it.

Lights and cameras are too small to be worth sharing and aren't instanced.  Two lanterns are two CLights.  A light on its own
has its own connection, and one carried by a character has a connection whose parent is the character's hand.

CScene holds three lists: instances, lights and cameras.  Everything else is reached through what uses it.


The rule
--------
A step builds RasterLib's types from the scene.  RasterLib never includes the scene's headers.

These stay out of RasterLib:
  Transforms with parents        CConnection's local and world matrices.
  Lights as objects              CLight.  The rasteriser is given plain values to shade with.
  Keyframes or time              CConnectionAnimation.
  A list of what's in the world  CScene.


What goes where
---------------
  Scene                  Drawn as                     Today
  CMesh                  CRasterMesh                  Done: CRasterMesh::Build
  CInstance's meshes     SRasterInstance, per mesh    BoardScene writes axes and offsets by hand
  CCamera+CConnection    CRasterCamera                Viewers set CRasterCamera up directly
  CLight                 CRasteriser's light values   Fixed: msLight and RASTER_AMBIENT
  CConnectionAnimation   (changes CConnection)        Nothing.  BoardScene sets a yaw


Steps
-----
Each is done when something needs it, with tests.

1. Remove CFakeTracker and TrackerTypes.  CScene becomes a CObject with its three lists.  CInstance holds its meshes, a
   connection slot for each, and its connections.  CLight and CCamera each get a Ptr<CConnection>.
2. CConnection gets a Ptr<CConnection> parent and updates: a world matrix from a local one and the parent's.
3. The step from a scene to SRasterInstances, one for each rigid mesh of each instance: axes and offset from the world matrix
   of the mesh's connection, in one scene-wide scale.
   Decide what happens to a mirrored matrix (mbParity), as SRasterInstance must turn and not mirror, and to one that scales.
4. The camera: CCamera and its connection set up a CRasterCamera.  ECameraType decides the projection, so ERasterProjection
   isn't set separately, and FOV becomes focal length.  Drag, Pan, Zoom and FitOrbit stay on CRasterCamera as viewer controls.
   Decide how they write back to the scene, if they should.
5. Lights: CRasteriser takes light values, in place of msLight and RASTER_AMBIENT, with what they are now as the default.
6. Move BoardScene onto a CScene: its models, the mainboard and boards standing in their sockets become instances and
   connections, and its yaw a connection or an animation.  This is what proves the steps above.
7. CConnectionAnimation: keyframes change connections, and the step in 3 runs again.  miConnectionIndex is a slot in the
   instance's connections, so that characters can share an animation.

Not planned: skinning.  CInstance's connections allow for it, as a skinned mesh's weights can use their slots, but RasterLib
has none, so it is new work of its own.


Open
----
Who owns the CRasterMeshes.  SRasterInstance's pcMesh is a raw pointer, and RasterLib can't see the scene, so something must
remember which CMesh was built into which CRasterMesh, so that a shared mesh is built once.

Where bone hierarchy lives.  CMeshSkin has the weights and inverse matrices, but nothing holds a skeleton that instances could
share.

Creating and removing an attached light.  Copying an instance doesn't bring its lantern along, so whatever creates the
character must also create the light and add it to the scene.  When the character is removed, something must remove the light,
or it stays lit where the hand was: its connection's parent keeps the hand's connection alive, but nothing updates it.
