Shape plan
==========

Five layers, each with one job, meeting in two places.  "Shape" means a CShape and nothing else.

  1. Value geometry        BaseLib    Plain structs that hold values and do the arithmetic.
  2. Point set algorithms  SupportLib Quickhull, and bounding fits when something needs them.
  3. Meshes                SupportLib CMesh and its details are the data; CMeshEditor and its modifiers make and change
                                      meshes.  Unchanged.
  4. Shapes                SupportLib CShape: solids that say how far a point is from their surface.
  5. Bridges               Shape to mesh (ProceduralLib), and mesh to shape (CMeshShape).

Nothing here is built yet but what each step says is done.


The user's decisions (2026-11-15)
---------------------------------
  The base is CShape, it is a CObject, and every shape extends it.
  The 2009 collision classes aren't repaired: the model below replaces them, and they're deleted.  They were pointer views onto
  a mesh's arrays (CSphere, CBox, CPlane, CHalfSpace, CTriangle, CPolygon, CConvexHull, ShapeHelper's C*Helper), index records
  (CShapeIndexed and C*Indexed) and a mesh's collision data (CMeshShape's CMeshTriangles, CMeshLeaves, CMeshBoundingBox and
  CMeshConvexHull), none of it called by anything but four thin tests, and broken: the hull generator's free lists were set up
  with their arguments the wrong way round once CFreeList::Init changed, it started from two flat faces that lose every point
  in their plane, and it only removed faces with points left to see.  The bugs found are listed in Found while reading, below.


1. Value geometry (BaseLib)
---------------------------
Structs beside SFloat32Vec3 and SFloat4x4: values, no pointers into other arrays, no CObject, so they go in arrays, free lists
and a renderer's per-frame data, and RasterLib (BaseLib and StandardLib only) can use them.

  SFloat32Plane     sNormal (unit), fDistance        Distance(p) = n.p - d, negative behind it: a half space's inside.
  SFloat32Sphere    sCenter, fRadius                 Distance, Contains, Volume, IntersectionVolume, fits to points.
  SFloat32Box       sCenter, asAxes[3] (unit,        An oriented box.  Distance (exact), Contains, Volume, GetCorner.
                    longest first), afHalfLengths[3]
  SFloat32Bounds    sMinimum, sMaximum               An axis aligned box: Empty, Add a point or bounds, Center, Size,
                                                     Contains, Distance (exact).  What CShape::GetBounds gives.
  SFloat32Triangle  asPositions[3]                   Normal, Plane, Area, Center, ClosestPoint, Distance (unsigned).

The rules, the same as CShape's: Distance is negative inside, positive outside, and exactly zero is outside (Contains is
Distance < 0).  A triangle has no inside, so its Distance is unsigned.  Only what something needs is written; ray tests,
segments and the like wait.

CCookedMesh's msMin and msMax and RasterLib's integer bounds could become SFloat32Bounds later.  Not part of this plan.


2. Point set algorithms (SupportLib)
------------------------------------
CQuickHull: points to their convex hull, as faces (coplanar triangles merged into polygons) with their planes and the
indices of the points they're made of.  Written fresh: it starts from a tetrahedron, a point is outside a face by a tolerance
scaled to the points' extent, every face a new point can see is removed, and flat or degenerate sets fail cleanly.  Tests: a
cube with points inside and on its faces, a turned box, random clouds against a brute force check that every point is behind
every face, and a flat set.

Bounding fits (a minimal sphere, an oriented box fitted to the hull) are written when collision or culling needs them.


3. Meshes (SupportLib)
---------------------
CMesh and its details are the data, and CMeshEditor and its modifiers make and change meshes, as they do now.  The procedural
mesh shapes (CMeshBoxEditor, CMeshSphereEditor and the others) stay mesh editors and aren't CShapes: each primitive CShape pairs
with one (section 4), and meshes and shapes meet only through the bridges (section 5).

The one change is a name, so a shape is only ever a CShape: MeshPolygonShape.h (cutting a polygon into faces for the OBJ and
scene readers, and walking round them for the writers) becomes MeshPolygonCut.h.  VectorShapes and GeometryMap are 2D outlines
for the vector rasteriser and stay as they are.


4. Shapes
---------
  class CShape : public CObject
  {
  public:
      virtual float32  Distance(SFloat32Vec3* psPosition) = 0;
      virtual void     Gradient(SFloat32Vec3* psDest, SFloat32Vec3* psPosition);  //Central differences unless overridden.
      virtual void     GetBounds(SFloat32Bounds* psBounds) = 0;
      virtual bool     IsExact(void);  //Distance is the distance, not only a bound.
              bool     Contains(SFloat32Vec3* psPosition);  //Distance < 0.
  };

Every shape is a solid.  Distance may be a bound: never more than the true distance.  Gradient needn't be a unit vector, and a
shape that can give it exactly should, as central differences across a crease average its faces.  Bounds may be infinite (a
half space); the isosurface is then given a box by its caller.

  Shape          Holds                                      Distance                         Mesh editor
  CSphere        SFloat32Sphere                             exact                            CMeshSphereEditor, Geosphere
  CBox           SFloat32Box                                exact                            CMeshBoxEditor
  CCylinder      ends, radius                               exact, capped                    CMeshCylinderEditor
  CCone          ends, two radii                            exact, capped                    CMeshConeEditor
  CTube          ends, two radii (a pipe's walls)           exact                            CMeshTubeEditor
  CTorus         centre, axis, two radii                    exact                            CMeshTorusEditor
  CCapsule       ends, radius                               exact                            none
  CHalfSpace     SFloat32Plane                              exact, infinite bounds           none
  CConvexHull    its faces' SFloat32Planes and its corners  exact inside, bound outside      none (made by CQuickHull)
  CMeshShape     a Ptr<CMesh> and its own search structure  exact                            the mesh itself

  CShapeUnion          Ptrs to two or more shapes      min: a bound
  CShapeIntersection   Ptrs to two or more shapes      max: a bound
  CShapeSubtraction    Ptrs to two shapes              max(a, -b): a bound
  CShapeSmoothUnion    Ptrs to two shapes, a radius    Quilez's polynomial smooth min: a bound
  CShapeTransform      a Ptr to one shape, a matrix    the shape's, at the point turned into its frame, scaled back by the
                       and its inverse                  smallest scale, so an uneven scale is a bound

A primitive is placed by its own values, as its mesh editor is, and CShapeTransform moves a combination.  Combinations hold
their children as Ptrs, so a shape is a graph like a scene, rooted by whatever holds it.  There are no open shapes: a mesh's
faces are already triangles, and SFloat32Triangle does their arithmetic.


5. Bridges
----------
Shape to mesh, in ProceduralLib: one source modifier that meshes any CShape.  A primitive is made by its own mesh editor with
the same values, exactly; anything else goes through the isosurface (Isosurface.txt).

Mesh to shape: CMeshShape is a closed mesh's distance: the closest face through a bounding volume hierarchy over its faces,
inside by winding number or pseudo normals (Baerentzen and Aanaes 2005).  It holds a Ptr<CMesh> and builds its hierarchy, and
its hull and bounds if wanted, in its own arrays, again when the mesh's change counters move.  It never writes into the mesh.
The old CMeshShape, which added its spheres' centres and its box's axes to the mesh's own positions and normals, goes.


Steps
-----
Each step has its tests, and TestBaseLib, TestSupportLib and their 65 suites pass after it.

1. Value geometry: SFloat32Plane, SFloat32Sphere, SFloat32Box, SFloat32Bounds and SFloat32Triangle in BaseLib, with
   TestBaseLib's tests, which take over the cases of TestSphereShape, TestHalfSpace and TestTriangleShape.  Done (eb0b0ed89).
   The sphere around points is the one centred on their average, which holds them all; the 2009 "best fit" (the two points
   furthest apart) didn't always.
2. Delete the 2009 classes: CSphere, CBox, CPlane, CHalfSpace, CTriangle, CPolygon, CConvexHull and its generator,
   ShapeHelper, ShapesIndexed, CMeshShape, CMeshTriangles, CMeshLeaves, CMeshBoundingBox and CMeshConvexHull, and their tests.
   Rename MeshPolygonShape.h.  Done: TestSphereShape, TestHalfSpace, TestPolygon and TestTriangleShape went with them, and
   BaseLib's RemoveAtNoDeallocate(Count), which only the hull generator used.
3. CQuickHull.  Done.  A point is outside a face by more than 1e-5 of how far the points reach along x, y and z together
   (QUICK_HULL_TOLERANCE).  The faces a new point sees are found across neighbours from the one it was furthest in front of,
   so they always join up; the triangles of one plane are merged into a face, its corners in order, with Newell's plane.
   TestQuickHull: a cube with points inside and on its faces and edges (six faces of four, only its corners are corners), a
   turned box, a cloud of 200 points and 120 on a sphere (all corners) checked by brute force and Euler, and points too few,
   flat, in a line or in one place.  Removing only the face a point was given to, not every face it sees, fails them.
4. CShape, with CSphere, CBox and CHalfSpace.  Tests: distances, gradients against central differences away from creases,
   bounds, Contains at exactly zero, and Save and Load.
   Done.  Each holds its value struct (msSphere, msBox, msPlane) as fields Class() names, so the object machinery saves and
   loads it; OMalloc<CSphere>(&sCenter, fRadius) and the like make one.  The sphere's and box's gradients are units (the
   sphere's is nothing at its centre); the half space's is its normal, and its bounds are as large as a float32 goes.  CShape's
   own Gradient steps SHAPE_GRADIENT_STEP (1e-4) of its bounds' longest side, or of 1.  TestShape; a chunk file holds the
   object written to it last, so each shape is saved to a file of its own.
5. CCylinder, CCone, CTube, CTorus and CCapsule.  Each also tested against its editor's mesh: every corner of the mesh made
   with the same values is on its surface, within a tolerance.
   Done.  They're CRevolvedShapes: a start, a unit axis and a length, with a point turned into its profile's plane (how far
   from the axis, how far along it) where each gives a 2D distance and gradient, which turning a profile keeps exact.  The
   cylinder's and tube's profiles are rectangles, the cone's a trapezoid (Quilez's capped cone, its gradient on the surface the
   nearest feature's normal), the torus's a circle and the capsule's a line less its radius.  Each takes the values its mesh
   editor does, in its order; the torus's length is nothing.  TestRevolvedShape along a tilted axis; a point exactly on a
   surface is only exactly on it along an axis, so that's where zero being outside is tested.
6. The combinations.  Tests: each is a bound (never more than a brute force distance to its surface sampled finely), a graph
   of them is freed with what holds it, and Save and Load.
   Done.  The union and intersection are CShapeGroups (a CArray<CShape>, the nearest or furthest at each point giving the
   distance and gradient); the subtraction's gradient is the cut's turned round where the cut wins; the smooth union is
   Quilez's polynomial smooth min in its blended form, whose gradient is the same blend of the two, exactly, and whose bounds
   grow by a quarter of its radius, as far as the blend can fill the seam.  The transform scales back by the smallest scale
   along its matrix's axes, not the largest as this plan first said: a world distance is at least the smallest scale times
   the shape's own, so only that is a bound; it's exact for an even scale.  TestShapeCombination checks each against a grid
   sampled surface (bisected where Contains changes), which fails, as the direct checks do, with the largest scale.
7. CConvexHull, from CQuickHull.  Done: its faces' planes (BaseLib's CArrayFloat32Plane) and its corners, saved by its own Save
   and Load as CMeshPositions saves its array.  Its distance is the furthest plane's, its gradient that plane's normal, and
   its bounds its corners'; points CQuickHull can't make a hull of make it nothing.  TestConvexHull: a box's points make a hull
   whose distance is the box's inside, exactly, and no more outside, and which the grid sampled surface check finds a bound;
   its planes, corners and distances are the same saved and read back.  The shape tests share one TestShapeRandom.
8. The isosurface and the shape to mesh modifier (Isosurface.txt).
   Done, in Isosurface.txt's sub-steps 8.1 to 8.8: ProceduralLib's marching cubes baseline, dual contouring on a uniform grid and
   on a balanced octree, manifold where sheets meet, and CMeshShapeEditor, a source modifier holding its CShape (modifiers are
   CObjects now).  A primitive is meshed by its own editor through CShape::AddMesh.  BaseLib has SFloat32Symmetric3x3, the vertex
   fit's eigen solver.
9. CMeshShape, when collision or picking needs it.


Found while reading the 2009 classes
------------------------------------
For the record, as the classes went rather than being fixed.  Step 1's TestFloat32 tests check the triangle's corner, the
sphere holding a triangle, and the box around points reading past the last and setting no centre.  CQuickHull's tests (step 3)
are to check what the hull generator got wrong.  The rest (the box's best fit, the leaves, the mesh's hull and bounding box) have
no new code yet; whatever replaces them (the bounding fits, CMeshShape) is to be tested against them when it's written.
  CTriangle::Set from nine numbers took the first corner's z for the second's.
  CSphere::Contains(CTriangle*) compared squared distances with the square root of the radius.
  CBox::SetFromPointsAxisAligned read the point after the last on every pass; neither box fit set the centre; the best fit took
  its axes from the rotation's rows, where v * M makes them its columns.
  CConvexHull's polygons pointed at normals the generator freed.  CMeshConvexHull looked their indices up among the mesh's
  normals, where they weren't, after copying in every normal the generator ever made.
  CMeshLeaves gave a triangle no normal and then worked out its plane from it, and never recorded a leaf's faces.
  CPolygonIndexed didn't save its normal or whether it was convex.
  CMeshBoundingBox fitted the box with a placeholder centre among the points, through a pointer the placeholder's Add could
  move.
  The hull generator's free lists, its flat start, its third point (measured from a plane through the origin) and its face
  removal, above.
