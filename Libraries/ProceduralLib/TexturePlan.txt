Texture generator plan
======================

Making the solid textures a bake reads (SupportLib/UnwrapPlan.txt, Bake): noise, the operators that combine textures, and
materials built from them.  It comes before the virtual texture (SupportLib/VirtualTexturePlan.txt), whose pages are baked from
these and whose design needs to know what a texel holds and what one costs to make.  Steps 1 to 38 are built (see Steps): what
is left is under Afterwards.  What is decided and what is only proposed is said at each point.


The user's decisions (2026-11-16)
---------------------------------
  Texture generators are next, not the virtual texture.
  A sample holds colour, height, shine, gloss and glow (What a texture gives): all five carried from the start, as carrying
  one is an operation or so an operator against a noise's hundreds.
  Solid textures only: every texture is a function of a 3D point.  A second family of 2D operators (as .werkkzeug's) waits
  until a scene needs what a solid can't do (see What a solid can't do).


What there was (before step 1)
------------------------------
  CSolidTexture (SupportLib): Colour(position, normal, width) gives an ARGB32 at a point in the world, through a placement
  (origin, axes, scale) to the texture's own TextureColour.  The width is the texel's, in world units, so a texture finer than
  a texel gives its average.
  CSolidChecker, the only texture: box filtered cubes of two colours.
  BakeMeshTexture and CMeshBake: one sample at each texel's centre, every mip level baked from the texture (never shrunk),
  gutters dilated, each face's old diffuse colour a tint.  The result is the material's diffuse image and its levels.
  CMaterial has image slots for bump and specular (MI_Bump, MI_Specular) that neither renderer reads; RenderLib reads only the
  diffuse image's levels (TextureLevels).
  BaseLib has no noise.  ProceduralLib has the isosurface and nothing of textures.


How .kkrieger did it, and what's taken from it
----------------------------------------------
.kkrieger's textures (Farbrausch's .werkkzeug 3) are stored as how they were made: a stack of operators, each a few bytes of
parameters, run at load.  A few generators (flat, Perlin, cells, gradient, glowing rectangles, bricks, text), many filters
(colour adjust, range, blur, rotate, distort, mask, merges), and lighting from heights (a normals operator, then a bump operator
that lights the colour).  All of it on 2D images that tile, 16 bits a channel.

Taken:
  The operator graph.  A texture is a small graph of objects, each a CSolidTexture holding the textures it reads.  The graph
  is what's saved; texels can always be made again from it.
  Height beside colour, as their best surfaces come from heights lit.
  Floats between operators (their 16 bits), so a long chain doesn't band.
  Range (a colour ramp) and mask as the operators most used.

Not taken: 2D images and tiling.  Ours are sampled a point at a time on the surface, every texel its own.


What a texture gives
--------------------
A sample, not a colour (decided, and built in step 1):

  struct SSolidSample
  {
      SFloat32Vec4  sColour;     //Red, green, blue and alpha, 0 to 1 but not clamped between operators.
      float32       fHeight;     //How far the surface is raised, in the texture's units; 0 where it has none.
      float32       fShine;      //How strong its highlight is, 0 to 1 (the material's shininess base).
      float32       fGloss;      //How tight its highlight is, 0 (broad) to 1 (a point) (the material's shininess power).
      float32       fGlow;       //How much of its colour it gives off as light, 0 to 1 (the material's emissive colour).
  };

CSolidTexture's virtual is TextureSample(position, normal, width, sample); Sample gives it at a point in the world, and Colour
stays, clamping the sample's colour to an ARGB32, so the bake and its tests are as they were.  A texture that is only a value
(noise) gives it as a grey colour and as the height, so any texture plugs into any operator, as .werkkzeug's images did.  The
one number an operator reads from a sample (a ramp's place, a mask's amount) is its colour's brightness.

Only colour is baked at first.  Height is used from step 32, where glow is too: a glowing texel isn't darkened by baked
shading, and is a light when the virtual texture bakes lighting.  Shine and gloss are a pair (wet stone and polished metal
both shine; one's highlight is broad, the other's a point) carried so operators needn't change when a renderer has highlights
from an image; neither does yet.  Each bakes to a byte a texel when something reads it, and to nothing until then.

Left out: opacity (the colour's alpha), a normal (made from the height in the bake; both would disagree), ambient occlusion
(worked out from the height and the shape, step 38), metalness (physically based rendering, which neither renderer does).


Where it lives
--------------
  BaseLib        Noise: plain arithmetic on floats, no objects (Noise.h).  Other libraries will want it (terrain, the
                 isosurface's shapes).
  SupportLib     CSolidTexture, the sample and the bake, as now.
  ProceduralLib  Every generator and operator, and the materials built from them.


Noise (built: steps 5 to 11)
----------------------------
  Hash           An integer lattice point (and a seed) to 32 bits, by multiplying and xoring; no permutation table, so no
                 256 period and nothing to initialise.
  Gradient       Perlin's improved noise (2002) in 3D: a gradient at each lattice point from the hash, the quintic fade,
                 -1 to 1, 0 at lattice points, its mean 0.  Its gradient is returned with it (the same sums, differentiated);
                 nothing uses it yet.
  Cellular       Worley's (1996): one jittered point in each lattice cube; the nearest two points' distances (F1, F2) and
                 the nearest's own number, from the cubes round the point.  F1 is pebbles and scales, F2 - F1 cracks and
                 mortar, the hash a colour for each cell.
  Fractal sums   Octaves of gradient noise, each twice the frequency and half the size (fBm); with absolute values
                 (turbulence, Perlin's marble and fire); ridged (1 - |n|, squared: mountains, veins).

Filtering: each noise takes the width of the texel looking at it and gives its average over that, near enough: an octave is
its mean plus what a texel that wide keeps of the rest (NoiseKept, measured), so an octave much finer than the texel is just
its mean and isn't worked out.  A coarse mip level is then the fine level's blur.  Cellular noise goes to its means the same
way.


Operators (built: steps 12 to 23 and 35 to 38)
----------------------------------------------
Each a CSolidTexture in ProceduralLib, named CSolid..., its inputs Ptr<CSolidTexture> fields (those with inputs are
CSolidOperators).  A texture can feed several operators (the graph isn't a tree); it can't feed itself.

  Generators     CSolidFlat        One sample everywhere.
                 CSolidGradient    A value rising from a plane, or out from an axis (rings: wood) or a point; once, as a
                                   saw or as a wave.
                 CSolidNoise       A fractal sum: kind (a sum, turbulence, ridged), octaves, seed.
                 CSolidCells       Cellular: F1, F2 - F1 or each cell's own value.
                 CSolidBricks      A lattice of boxes with mortar, rows offset, each brick's own value (step 35).
                 CSolidChecker     As it is, in SupportLib.

  Point          CSolidRamp        The input's value through a table of keys (value and a sample), a straight blend
                                   between them.  Noise through a ramp is most materials.
                 CSolidAdjust      Hue, saturation, contrast, brightness; invert.
                 CSolidMerge       Two inputs added, multiplied, the lesser, the greater, the difference.
                 CSolidMask        Two inputs blended, a third saying how much of each.

  Position       Placement         Every texture has one already.  An input is sampled at the operator's own point, so its
                                   placement is inside its parent's: a graph is placed in the world once, at its top.
                 CSolidWarp        Its input sampled at the point moved by a second input's values (at the point and at
                                   two points far from it, one for each axis), by an amount in units.  Marble's veins,
                                   wood's wobble.

  Surface        CSolidSlope       How nearly the normal faces a direction, as a value: moss on top, dust in the lee.
                 CSolidCavity      From a CShape's distances near the point: dirt in hollows, wear on edges (step 38).
                                   A solid texture can do this where a 2D one never could, as the shape is to hand.

A material is a function that builds a graph (MakeSolidMarble(seed) returning its top texture), not a class: the graph is
ordinary objects, saved and edited as they are.


Filtering and operators that aren't straight (built: steps 28 to 30)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Noise is filtered by its width.  But a ramp of a filtered noise isn't the filtered ramp of the noise: a hard key in a ramp, a
mask's edge, min and max all put back detail finer than the texel, and the mean moves too.  Two remedies were proposed:

  Soft keys      A ramp's step made no sharper than the input's change across the texel.  Measured, and not built (step 29).
  More samples   The bake takes several samples in a texel and averages.  Built (step 30): one at level 0, twice as many
                 each way at each level after, up to four.


Height (built: steps 32 to 34)
------------------------------
The bake turns height into a normal: it finds how the height rises across each texel from the samples it takes there, and
tilts the surface's normal by that slope (Blinn's bump mapping, 1978).  No image neighbours are used, so seams don't show.

What it's for, in order:
  Baked shading.  The colour is lit by the tilted normal from a fixed direction as it's baked (.werkkzeug's bump operator).
  It needs nothing of the renderers and is most of the look.  It's wrong when the light moves, and it is what the virtual
  texture's baked lighting (its step 6) does properly.
  A normal image.  The tilted normals baked to the material's bump slot, in the mesh's own space (every texel is unique, so no
  tangent frames are needed, and none meet at seams).  Used when a renderer lights each pixel; neither does yet.


What a solid can't do
---------------------
  Blur, sharpen, grow and shrink by a chosen amount: they need a texel's neighbours.  A shape's roundness is made in its
  generator instead.
  Text and loaded pictures.
  Patterns that follow the surface.  Bricks are a lattice of boxes in space, right on walls square to it; a slanted or curved
  surface cuts the lattice into slivers.
Bricks on slanted surfaces and text are what would bring in 2D operators, projected by the normal.  Not planned.


Steps
-----
Each with its tests: noise in TestBaseLib, the rest in TestProceduralLib.  From step 16 the tests of textures draw a gallery:
a sphere and a box baked with each texture, drawn by the rasteriser to Output/Gallery/<name>.png, to be looked at, as the
look is judged by eye.  What was found building a step is under it.

The sample
1.  SSolidSample: colour, height, shine, gloss and glow as floats.  Done.
2.  CSolidTexture::Sample and the TextureSample virtual; Colour kept on top for the bake.  Done.
3.  CSolidChecker moved to it.  Done.
4.  TestMeshBakeSample; the bake's and the textured scene's results unchanged.  Done.
    A sample is also its eight numbers in a row (Channels), so operators that treat every channel alike loop over them, and
    Blend moves it towards another.  The one number an operator reads from a sample (Value) is its colour's brightness, not
    its height, so a texture adjusted or ramped is a mask as it looks; a texture that is only a number gives it as a grey and
    as the height (InitValue).

Noise in BaseLib (Noise.h)
5.  NoiseHash: a lattice point and a seed to 32 bits, no table.  Done.
6.  GradientNoise in 3D (Perlin's improved), -1 to 1.  Done.
7.  Its gradient, returned with the value.  Done: the corners' gradients blended, and the fade's slope times the blend at
    the cube's far face less that at its near one.
8.  CellularNoise (Worley's): the nearest two distances and the cell's own number.  Done: exact, as the 27 cubes round the
    point aren't always enough (a point two cubes away can be nearer than the nearest's found): if the second nearest is
    past the nearest face of the block searched, the block a cube bigger is searched (1.7% of points).
9.  FractalNoise: octaves summed, as turbulence, ridged.  Done: over the sum of the octaves' strengths, so each kind keeps
    its range whatever the octaves; each octave's lattice is moved, so their zeros don't all fall on the first's lattice
    points; the sum's gradient too.
10. Filtering by width.  Done: NoiseKept is how much of a noise's difference from its mean is left over a width, fitted to
    gradient noise averaged over squares (0.87 at half a unit, 0.58 at one, 0.15 at two): 1 - smoothstep to two and a half
    units.  An octave is its mean plus what's kept of the rest, so octaves too fine aren't worked out, and turbulence,
    ridges and cells go to their measured means (0.2204, 0.6322; 0.5201 and 0.7167 for the nearest two distances), not to 0.
    Against the average of 36 samples across half a unit: 0.024 root mean square for a sum, 0.033 turbulence, 0.051 ridged.
11. TestNoise.  Done: 2088 checks; on the 65C64 and W65C816 too (Libc65 has no float.h, so none is used).

Generators in ProceduralLib
12. CSolidFlat: one sample everywhere.  Done.
13. CSolidGradient: a value rising with distance from a plane, an axis or a point; once, as a saw or as a wave.  Done: its
    mean over the texel's width along the distance, exactly, from the area under each repeat (as CSolidChecker's box filter).
14. CSolidNoise: a fractal sum as a value, 0 to 1 (a sum about a half).  Done.
15. CSolidCells: the nearest distance, how much further the second is (0 along cells' edges), or the cell's own.  Done.
16. The gallery (TestProceduralLib's TextureGallery).  Done.  TestShapeScene's scene helpers moved to ShapeSceneHelper for it.

Point operators (each a CSolidOperator, whose Input samples an input at the operator's point, black if there's none)
17. CSolidRamp: the input's value through a table of keys, each a full sample.  Done: keys kept in order of value; between
    two a straight blend of every channel; two at one value a hard edge.
18. CSolidAdjust: hue, saturation, contrast, brightness, invert.  Done: hue is the colour turned about the line of greys,
    so no conversion to hue and back.
19. CSolidMerge: add, multiply, lesser, greater, difference.  Done: every channel alike.
20. CSolidMask: two inputs blended by a third.  Done: where the mask is all one input the other isn't sampled.
21. A graph saved, loaded, and sampling the same.  Done: TestSolidGraphSaveLoad (a mask of a merge of two ramps and a flat,
    by a third ramp).

Warp and the first materials (SolidMaterials.h: each a function building a graph)
22. Placement inside a parent: an input is sampled at its operator's point.  Done with CSolidOperator::Input;
    TestSolidPlacementInside.
23. CSolidWarp: the input sampled at a point moved by a second input.  Done: each axis's move is the amount times how far
    the second's value is from a half, doubled, the y and z values read far from the point so one noise bends every way.
24. Marble: a wave of bands along x warped by a noise (0.9 of a unit: the bands are 0.7 apart, so they fold right over),
    through a ramp pale for most of a band and dark at its crest.  Done.
25. Wood: a saw of rings round z, eight to the unit, wobbled by a broad noise; a fine noise multiplies in grain.  Done.
26. Stone: two noises through ramps, one or the other by cell, times dark sunk cracks along a second set of cells' edges
    (themselves warped a little, so they aren't straight).  Done.
27. Rust: steel and rust, the rust where a ridged noise is high.  Done.
    All four looked right in the gallery the first time they were drawn; the numbers in them are that first guess.

Filtering and cost measured (TestSolidFiltering; TEXTURE_FILTER_REPORT in the environment prints its tables)
28. Each material's mip levels against the truth.  Done.  A texel's truth is the mean of 64 samples across it; the error
    is the root mean square, over 144 texels on a plane and red, green and blue (0 to 1), of how far a bake is from that.
    One sample a texel, at widths of a 64th of a unit, a 32nd ... a half:
      Checker, saw   0 (they average themselves exactly)
      Noise (sum)    0.001 0.002 0.003 0.006 0.012 0.020
      Cells (cell)   0.016 0.019 0.026 0.050 0.083 0.096   (0.046 0.073 0.095 ... before step 29)
      Marble         0.003 0.007 0.020 0.039 0.060 0.091
      Wood           0.025 0.030 0.033 0.030 0.021 0.010
      Stone          0.017 0.039 0.062 0.079 0.077 0.097
      Rust           0.005 0.012 0.023 0.044 0.067 0.100
    The generators are near the truth.  The materials are a tenth of the whole range out at coarse levels: partly edges a
    ramp or a mask puts back, mostly the mean moving (a marble's veins fade, so coarse levels are paler than fine ones).
29. Softening, where the measure shows flicker.  Done for cells: the edge between the two nearest points' cells is the
    plane half way between them, and a texel within half its width of it is partly the second cell, so a cell's number
    crosses an edge in a texel's width (CellularNoise; a third of the error at fine levels).  Soft keys in the ramp were
    measured and not built.  They would average the ramp over the values its input takes across the texel, which mends
    one case: a steep key on an input that changes evenly (stone's cracks, 0.020 at the finest level).  The other edges
    aren't that: a saw's fall through a ramp (wood's rings, 0.024) and a ramp of a cell's number (stone's patches, 0.027)
    are two values with nothing between, and soft keys need the input's slope, two more samples of it for every ramp.
    Step 30 mends all of them for less.
30. Several samples in a texel in the bake.  Done: SMeshBakeSettings' iSamples each way, spread evenly across the texel
    (a texel across or down is the same step in the world all over a face), each as wide as its share, their mean the
    texel.  CMeshBake's level 0 is one sample; each level after has twice as many each way, up to miSamples (4), so
    coarse levels are made of samples as fine as level 0's.  The errors as it bakes its levels:
      Marble         0.003 0.002 0.001 0.002 0.005 0.014
      Wood           0.025 0.012 0.006 0.009 0.012 0.010
      Stone          0.017 0.008 0.004 0.008 0.016 0.022
      Rust           0.005 0.003 0.001 0.003 0.006 0.011
    for about two and a half times the samples (level 0's again for each of levels 1 and 2, little after).  Level 0's
    own errors are detail finer than a texel (wood's grain) and hard edges, as any texture's finest level has.
31. The cost of a sample of each material at a 64th of a unit, in microseconds, Release x64 on this machine: a noise of
    six octaves 0.16, cells 0.12, marble 0.46, wood 0.42, stone 0.72, rust 0.30.  A page of 128 by 128 texels with its
    share of coarser levels (2.5 times) is 5 to 30 milliseconds on one thread: a page can be baked when it's first
    wanted if a frame needn't wait for it, which the virtual texture's baking in the background is for.

Height (in the bake: SupportLib's MeshBake)
32. The bake's tilted normal.  Done.  The height's slope on a face comes from the texel's own samples (the best fit
    through their heights, so two each way at least), not from points a texel away as first planned: the samples are
    there already, and it's right across seams as that was.  The normal less the slope, made a unit, is the tilted
    normal.  A texture's height is in its own units, so CSolidTexture::Sample multiplies it by the texture's scale: a
    texture placed half the size is raised half as far and slopes as it did.
33. Baked shading.  Done: SMeshBakeSettings' fRelief and sLight.  The colour is times 1 + fRelief times how much more of
    the light the tilted normal faces than the smooth one (so a flat height, or none, changes nothing, and it's the
    change a renderer's own lighting of the smooth surface lacks); a texel that glows is changed that much less.
    CMeshBake's mfRelief is 1 and its light above, to the left and in front.  Not done: darkening in hollows, which
    is step 38's.
34. The normal image.  Done: BakeMeshTexture gives it with the colours (x, y and z as bytes, in the mesh's space), and
    CMeshBake with mbNormals puts it and its levels in the material's bump image.  Off unless asked for, as neither
    renderer reads it.

Structured patterns
35. CSolidBricks.  Done: boxes a size apart along x, y and z with mortar between, each row moved a share of a length
    along x from the row below and each layer in depth from the layer in front (a half is running bond on a wall's
    face and on its top).  Its numbers are a brick's own, and how far inside its brick the point is (0 in the mortar),
    which through a ramp is a mask with a bevel.  MakeSolidBricks: each brick its own red, proud of pale mortar.  It
    doesn't average itself: the bake's samples do.
36. Tiles.  Done: the same lattice with no offset; MakeSolidTiles is cubes a quarter of a unit, cream or terracotta by
    their own numbers, glazed, with dark grout.
    Both are only right on faces square to the lattice, so the gallery places them along its box
    (TextureGalleryAlign), a little off the box's corner: a face that lies in one of the lattice's planes is all mortar.

Surface operators
37. CSolidSlope.  Done: 1 where the normal is along its direction (up, as it starts), a half square to it, 0 against.
38. CSolidCavity.  Done: it holds a CShape and asks its distance a reach out from the surface along the normal (less
    than the reach where the surface is hollowed) and a reach in (less deep than the reach under an edge); a half on
    a flat or evenly curved surface, towards 0 in hollows, towards 1 on edges.  The shape is in the space the texture
    is placed in, so the texture takes its point back out of its own space (CSolidTexture::FromOwn).  On a mesh of
    flat faces a curve's value varies a little across each face (a face's middle is inside the true surface), which
    shows only when the raw value is baked with relief, never through a ramp's keys.
    MakeSolidWeathered(material, shape, seed) puts them to work: the material paler along the shape's edges, earth in
    its hollows and moss in patches on what faces up.

Afterwards
39. Looking: HCP65-Sculpt, a program of its own in its own repository (HCP65-Sculpt/SculptPlan.txt): a 3D view over a
    node editor for a material's graph, as Blender's.  Built (its plan's steps 1 to 24 say how).  What it needed of this
    library, all here now: every operator gives something with an input missing (TestSolidOperatorsUnlinked; an input's
    own value is a CSolidFlat linked to it); CSolidTexture::Changed, for fields set from outside; CSolidChannel and
    CSolidCombine (one channel of a sample as a grey; a sample of a colour and four brightnesses); and a material as a
    configuration, the graph itself in a file (SolidMaterialFile.h: WriteSolidMaterial and ReadSolidMaterial).
    SolidMaterials' functions stay as the code way of making one.  The materials' numbers are still first guesses:
    they're to be tuned in Sculpt.
40. The virtual texture (SupportLib/VirtualTexturePlan.txt).
41. Loaded textures on a second UV layer (SupportLib/UnwrapPlan.txt, step 10).


Open
----
Whether colours are in the display's space or linear between operators.  Proposed: as the renderers take them now, looked at
again with baked lighting.
Whether a texture read by several operators is worked out once for a point (each texture remembering its last point and
sample) or each time.  Each time, for now: step 31's costs are a few tenths of a microsecond a sample.
Baking on more than one thread.  A sample has no side effects, so texels can be shared out; left to the virtual texture's
background baking.
Shine, gloss and glow are carried and not baked: nothing reads them (glow only holds off the relief's shading).
A CMeshBake's level 0 takes four samples a texel for the relief, where it took one: a bake is about five times the samples
it was.  A texture with no height (a checker) gains nothing from it; set mfRelief to 0 for one.
A graph can't be told from its top what it needs of the bake (whether it has height, whether it averages itself), so the
bake's samples are the same for every texture.
