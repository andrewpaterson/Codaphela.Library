CppCompilerLib: what the check of 2026-11-21 found
==================================================

Every finding of the code smell check of the library, numbered once from 1, and after them what was found the same
day by running, testing and measuring.  Written 2026-11-21 for whoever takes any of it on.  Nothing here is a plan:
ConsolidationPlan.txt is the plan for the shape of the code, and most of the largest findings are what its stages 6
to 10 are for.

How it was made, and how far to trust it

  - Findings 1 to 88 are six reviews, one for each area of the library, each made by reading the code and nothing
    else.  A reviewer built nothing and ran nothing, so where a finding says what goes wrong, that is "by reading"
    unless it says otherwise.  The text of each finding is the reviewer's, as it was reported; only the numbers are
    new (a reviewer's "finding 3" is the number it has here).
  - About forty of the reviews' particular claims (a file and line, a count, "no caller") were checked against the
    source afterwards, and all held.  The rest were not checked one by one.
  - The reviews read the library as it was in the early hours of 2026-11-21, at about commit 95556e055: 574 files,
    173,000 lines, 4,540 functions (half of them 10 lines or fewer, a tenth over 51, a hundredth over 152, the
    longest 276; 327 over 60 lines and 130 over 100).  Line numbers are that day's and have moved since: the
    clauses 3 to 14 fixes and DeclarationsSpeedPlan.txt's stages went in after.  Look for the name, not the line.
  - "Plan:" in a finding says whether ConsolidationPlan.txt already has it; "Confidence:" is the reviewer's own.
  - Where something has been run or changed since, a line "Since:" at the end of the finding says so.
  - Findings 89 to 106 were found by running programs, by the suite's coverage (g++, --coverage, commit b87a252bc)
    and by profiling and counting compiles; each says how.

The preprocessor, the tokeniser, the parser and the syntax tree
---------------------------------------------------------------

All files are in D:\gameengine\CommonLibraries\Libraries\CppCompilerLib. Read-only; nothing was built or run, so
every "consequence" below is from reading the code.

The syntax tree is the clean part: one X-macro (CPP_SYNTAX_KINDS, CPPSyntaxKind.h:26) makes the enum, names,
CreateKind and the visitor, and each node's VisitFields drives children, copying and the disk form, so visiting and
reading/writing are table-driven; only Init, Kill and PrettyPrint are hand-written per node. The parser is readable
but carries its context in members saved and put back by hand, and makes every node's bookkeeping by hand. The
preprocessor is the weakest part: three token forms, the same lexing redone five or six times, and a layer of
classes left from an older design that nothing uses. What a token is (spelling, operator class, precedence, literal
type) is spread over many switches.

1. A node's fields are listed three times by hand, and one list is already wrong
   Kind: one fact in several places (with a defect).
   Where: every CPPSyntax*.cpp has Init (76 nodes), Kill (27) and VisitFields (75). CPPSyntaxLambdaCapture.h:49,
   CPPSyntaxLambdaCapture.cpp:75-82.
   Evidence: I compared each node's members with its Init and VisitFields by script. One mismatch: `mbPack` of
   CCPPSyntaxLambdaCapture is set in Init, set by the parser (CppParserExpressions.cpp:1098) and read by
   CPPUnitLambdas.cpp:202, but VisitFields gives only meKind, mpcName, miName, mpcInitializer, mpcSymbol.
   Why it matters: VisitFields is what CPPSyntaxBlock writes and reads, and what CCPPSyntaxCopier copies
   (CPPUnitPacks.cpp:248, CPPUnitTemplates.cpp:815, CPPUnitLambdas.cpp:311). A `[args...]` capture read from a lazy
   header block or copied comes back with mbPack false. Nothing checks a new field against the list.
   Clean form: Init and Kill run through VisitFields (one CCPPSyntaxFields that nulls and Inits, one that Kills),
   so a field is listed once. Add mbPack now; the block magic must change with it.
   Plan: new (the plan leaves the node classes alone). Confidence: high on the omission, medium on the effect.
   Since: shown by running, and fixed in 51a96c492: VisitFields lists mbPack, and TestCPPSyntaxBlockEveryKind
   round-trips a source that has every kind of node and says which kind it lacks.  Init and Kill are still written
   by hand for each node.  The block's magic was changed later for another reason (CSB3, 18f0f7962); blocks are
   only kept in memory, so no old one is read.

2. Three token forms, with the lexing redone at each join
   Kind: duplicated work between the two token systems.
   Where: CPPToken family (PPToken.h, PPText.h), SPreprocessedToken (PreprocessedTokens.h:28-70), CCPPToken family
   (CppToken.h, CT*.h); CPPPreprocessor.cpp:626-827, 1736-1792, 2562-2610, 2805-2813; CppTokeniser.cpp:380-395.
   Evidence:
   - The line tokeniser makes punctuation one-character "decorators". ConvertDecorators (CPPPreprocessor.cpp:746)
     glues them into punctuators with PunctuatorLength every time a line is read (NextFileToken:563), for every
     unit.
   - The result is only PTK_Punctuator plus text, so the preprocessor compares strings (`IsPunctuator("(")`, 19
     times in CPPPreprocessor.cpp), and CCPPTokeniser::Punctuator looks the text up a third time for its
     ECppTokens.
   - Two more hand lexers sit in the same file: EmitPragmaPack (1757-1790) and LexSingleToken for pasting (2562).
   - A macro body is kept twice in CDefine (Define.h:42 mcReplacement, :54 macBody), converted lazily by the
     preprocessor through IsBodyConverted/SetBodyConverted (GetBody:2805).
   - Directive handlers use two forms: #define, #undef, #line, #pragma and #ifdef walk CPPTokens through
     CPreprocessorTokenParser; #if, #elif and #include convert to SPreprocessedToken first (3309, 3645).
   - CPreprocessorLineTokeniser keeps its state as seven "start" pointers of which one is set
     (PreprocessorLineTokeniser.h:36-42); `AddRelevantToken(); NullAll(); mszXStart = ...; MarkStart();` is
     repeated 12 times (15 NullAll calls).
   Why it matters: a lexical rule change has to be made in several lexers.
   Clean form: the file tokeniser makes SPreprocessedToken-shaped tokens directly (kind, text, flags, punctuator id
   from the one table). Directives, macro bodies, pasting and the C++ tokeniser all read that form. The line
   tokeniser's state is one start pointer and a kind.
   Plan: stage 10 says the four scanners "need reading first". This is that reading: CPreprocessorParser is a
   character cursor, CPreprocessorTokeniser splits lines and directives, CPreprocessorLineTokeniser lexes a line,
   CPreprocessorTokenParser is a cursor over CPPTokens. The plan does not name the second token form or the
   re-lexing: new. Confidence: high.

3. #if prints its tokens back to text and lexes them again
   Kind: same idea written twice; twin inconsistency.
   Where: CPPPreprocessor.cpp:3333-3344 (EvaluateCondition); PreprocessorExpression.cpp:27-51, 435-548, 578-616.
   Evidence: after expansion the tokens are appended to a CChars with spaces and given to
   CPreprocessorExpression::Evaluate(char*). That class has its own operator enum (PPO_*), its own precedence table
   (`giPPOperatorPrecedence`, line 51) and its own character-pair operator lexer (592-613). The parser has another
   precedence table (CCPPParser::GetBinaryPrecedence, CppParserExpressions.cpp:181). The pp-number rule is written
   three times and not alike: PPNumberLength (CPPPreprocessor.cpp:1712) and IsPPNumberContinuation
   (PreprocessorLineTokeniser.cpp:363) take a sign after e, E, p or P; Number()
   (PreprocessorExpression.cpp:510-527) allows e only when not hex.
   Why it matters: the evaluator knows nothing the token table knows. The alternative tokens (`and`, `or`, `not`,
   CppTokeniser.cpp:163-173) are plain identifiers to it, so `#if defined(A) and defined(B)` should end in
   "Unexpected text" (Evaluate:148).
   Clean form: evaluate over the SPreprocessedToken array with punctuator ids, with precedence taken from the one
   operator table.
   Plan: new. Confidence: high on structure, medium on the `and` consequence.

4. What an operator token is, is a switch wherever it is asked
   Kind: one fact listed in many places.
   Where and evidence:
   - Assignment operators: CppParserExpressions.cpp:101 and again :359-369 (IsDecltypeMemberCast), besides the four
     outside my area you named, and three more in the IDE (IDEConvertEnum.cpp:585, IDEEncapsulateField.cpp:235,
     IDEInlineVariable.cpp:228, IDERefactorUnit.h:169).
   - Binary precedence: CppParserExpressions.cpp:192-229.
   - IsOverloadableOperator (CppParserNames.cpp:269) is a list of exceptions plus `eToken < CT_Hash`, so it depends
     on enum order.
   - Type keywords: DeclSpecifierFlag, IsSimpleTypeKeyword, IsTypeStartKeyword
     (CppParserDeclarations.cpp:1062-1155).
   - The token set itself is in three places kept alike by position: enum ECppTokens (CppTokens.h:28),
     CPP_PUNCTUATORS (CPPPunctuatorList.h:28, "in ECppTokens order"), and the keyword rows typed out in
     CppTokeniser.cpp:55-159. A run-time check in every CCPPTokeniser::Init (CppTokeniser.cpp:226-237) reports "C++
     reserved words are out of order."
   Clean form: one X-macro table for all tokens (punctuators already have one) with columns for spelling, binary
   precedence, assignment's base operator and overloadable. The enum is generated from it. IsAssignment, Precedence
   and the rest are lookups that the parser, CCPPUnit, the index and the IDE share.
   Plan: new. Confidence: high.

5. The parser's context is members saved and put back by hand, in three overlapping lists
   Kind: state saved and restored around calls; hidden arguments.
   Where: CppParser*.cpp; CppParser.h:38-71; CppParserLazy.cpp:511-554.
   Evidence:
   - `bNoGreater = mbNoGreater; mbNoGreater = false; ... mbNoGreater = bNoGreater;` has 20 saves and 33 restores.
     ParseNewExpression restores at nine exits (CppParserExpressions.cpp:1409-1516). mbDeclaringTemplate gets the
     same treatment (CppParserClasses.cpp:568-602, CppParserDeclarators.cpp:481-521,
     CppParserExpressions.cpp:1024-1030).
   - mbMemberName and mbLambdaDeclarator are arguments passed through members: set before the call
     (CppParserExpressions.cpp:640-642, 993-996), read and cleared inside (CppParserNames.cpp:106-107,
     CppParserDeclarators.cpp:314).
   - "The state" exists three times: SCPPParserState (4 members), SCPPTentative (6), and LoadDeclaration's own 11
     saved members. LoadDeclaration does not save or reset miTemplates, mbLambdaDeclarator or miTemplateArguments,
     so a lazy declaration loaded inside a template or a lambda's parameters parses with the outer values. For
     example ParseFunctionDefinition's `IsSkimming() && (miTemplates == 0)` test (CppParserDeclarations.cpp:719)
     would see the outer miTemplates.
   Clean form: one context struct with a scoped setter that restores on every exit; LoadDeclaration swaps the whole
   struct; member-name and lambda-declarator become parameters.
   Plan: stage 10 covers the reshaping (token cursor, tentative parse as an object, one shared context). It does
   not name mbNoGreater's 53 sites, the hidden arguments or LoadDeclaration's omissions. Confidence: high on the
   evidence, medium on the omissions' effect.

6. An integer literal's type is decided twice, first by the host's sizes
   Kind: one fact derived twice; token classes that need kind plus cast.
   Where: CppTokeniser.cpp:467-500; CPPUnitExpressions.cpp:44-121, 775-800; CTNumber.h, CTInt.h, CTShort.h,
   CTLongLong.h.
   Evidence: CCPPTokeniser::Integer sorts literals "with 32 bit int and long and 64 bit long long, as on Windows"
   into CCTInt or CCTLongLong, with mbSigned, mbLong and mbDecimal. CCPPUnit::IntegerLiteralType then decides again
   from those flags and the target's int size (`mcTypes.msInt.iBytes < 4 ? 0x7FFF : 0x7FFFFFFF`). The classes are
   named for host storage: CCTShort is really "an L'x' or u'x' character", and CCTInt with mbChar32 a U'x'.
   Consumers switch on meType and cast (Literal, TokenConstant, CPPUnitFloat.cpp:92). CTNT_Byte is used nowhere.
   Clean form: one integer and one floating literal token holding what LiteralHelper parsed (value, base, suffixes,
   character encoding); the target decides the type once.
   Plan: new. Confidence: high.

7. CPPSyntaxBlock: scope and symbol records laid out twice, and Read does everything
   Kind: parallel lists; long function and parameter list; magic numbers; a rule copied from the parser.
   Where: CPPSyntaxBlock.cpp:955-999 against 1673-1711; Read 1516-1769; 1242-1248 and 1551-1553; 1663 against
   CppParserClasses.cpp:141.
   Evidence:
   - Nodes go through VisitFields, but a scope's and a symbol's fields are written out by hand in WriteAll and
     again, in the same order, in Read, and a third time in the header comment (CPPSyntaxBlock.h:66-69).
   - Read is 251 lines with 8 parameters and 30 locals. A CCPPSyntaxBlockReading object exists beside it but holds
     7 of the 10 working arrays; the other three are Read's locals, killed by hand at two exits. pcGlobal is
     derivable from pcTree.
   - The arrays' allocators are `GetBlockReadingMallocator(0)` to `(9)`, bare numbers against
     CPP_SYNTAX_BLOCK_READING_ARRAYS 10 (CPPSyntaxTree.h:53).
   - The reuse rule `pcSymbol->IsClass() && ... !(pcSymbol->mpcMembers && (pcSymbol->mpcMembers->mpcOwner !=
     pcSymbol))` is ParseClassSpecifier's, restated "as parsing does".
   Clean form: CCPPScope and CCPPSymbol give their fields to the same kind of field visitor; Read becomes steps of
   CCPPSyntaxBlockReading (header, externals, declared, scopes, symbols, nodes, tokens); an enum for the arrays;
   one "class already in this scope" function that the parser and the reader both call.
   Plan: new. The plan leaves the block's format alone; this changes no bytes. Confidence: high.

   Kind: dead code. Each was grepped across CommonLibraries, HCP65-IDE and HCP65.
   Evidence:
   - CDefineArguments (DefineArguments.cpp/.h, 119 lines): named nowhere outside its own two files.
   - CPPTokenReplacementsHolder: never created. IsTokenReplacementsHolder is never overridden to true, so its Copy
     does nothing and the branch at TokenHelper.cpp:93 can't be reached.
   - CPPBlock: never created. CPPBlockSet::AddBlock has no callers and mapcBlocks is always empty.
     `GetUnprocssedTokens` (misspelt) is named against "processed" blocks that no longer exist.
   - CPPConditional's mpcNext and msIndex, GetNext, GetTokenBlock: written by the tokeniser (NextConditional,
     mpcPrev, and the iBlock/iIndex parameters of TokeniseDirective and TokeniseHashConditional) and read by
     nobody.
   - CPPToken::IsUnknown and IsFile: no override, no caller.
   - CPPInclude: a subclass that adds only an Init.
   - EConditionalType: only CT_Else is ever tested (ConditionalStack.cpp:215). CT_EndIf is "Should never appear",
     and PushIf, PushIfDefined and PushIfNotDefined differ only in a value nobody reads.
   - ConvertDecorators returns a CPPToken* that its one caller ignores (CPPPreprocessor.cpp:697).
   - CPPReplacement::mbVariadic is only printed, and computed from the wrong number: `iReplacementNum ==
     iNumArguments - 1` counts uses, not the argument index (CPPPreprocessor.cpp:1123).
   - CCPPPreprocessor::EvaluateEquation is a one-line forwarder.
   Clean form: delete them.
   Plan: new. Confidence: high.
   Since: the suite's coverage agrees: no test calls a function of these classes (finding 96).

9. Every node's bookkeeping is done by the parse function
   Kind: the same few lines everywhere.
   Where: CppParser*.cpp.
   Evidence: `miFirstToken = CurrentToken()` 70 times, `miLastToken = PreviousToken()` 80 times, re-parenting by

   Plan: stage 10 gives the cursor Peek/Is/Expect but not node begin/finish: mostly new. Confidence: high.

The semantic layer: CCPPUnit and the classes round it
-----------------------------------------------------

The entity classes are in good shape: CCPPClass, CCPPFunction, CCPPVtable, CCPPClassLayout, CCPPExceptionSpec,
CCPPErrors, CCPPUnitData and CCPPContext are small, single-purpose and nearly free of GetKind. CCPPUnit is not. It
is still about 550 member functions in 13 files (my count of definitions; the brief's figure is 527), and stages 3
and 4 took out only the jobs that needed nothing from it. What remains is a dozen separate jobs sharing one set of
members, with three recurring habits: state passed through members rather than arguments, errors suppressed and
then recovered by running the work a second time, and small questions about syntax and types re-derived at each
call site. Textual duplication is indeed absent, but I found the same idea written two to nine times in different
words in most of the files. All findings below were read in the code; nothing was built or run.

   Kind: class doing several jobs; feature envy.
   Where: CPPUnit.h:349-942. Definitions per file: CPPUnit.cpp 172, Expressions 116, Templates 64, Objects 50,
   Classes 37, Lambdas 35, Check 25, Vtables 17, Packs 16, Overloads 11, Float 6, RangeFor 5, Labels 2.
   Evidence: grouping the header's members by job gives these, each of which would be a class.
   a. Diagnostics, about 20: Error, ErrorAt, ReportError, ReportErrorAt, ReportOnce, ClassError, ReportClassErrors,
   LambdaError, ConstantError, ConstantMessage, Warning, HasWarning, WarnDeprecated(Symbol), AppendMacroUse,

   EnumeratorValue, TypeTrait(Value), all of CPPUnitFloat.cpp.
   c. Warnings, 12 functions at CPPUnitExpressions.cpp:3041-3715: ConstantOverflow, ConversionOverflow,
   WideningArithmetic, NarrowMultiply, ShiftCount, ComparisonRange, ComparisonAtLimit, LoopCounter*. The plan's

   f. Lambdas, 35.
   g. Class data writer, about 25: vtables, VTT, type info, typeid labels and type names (CPPUnitVtables.cpp,
   CPPUnitObjects.cpp:895-1232).
   h. Name-to-class lookup, about 15 (finding 22).
   i. Class hierarchy questions, 9 (finding 21).
   j. Static data and start-up/exit routines: GlobalInitialiser, GlobalStartUpRoutines, BoundTemporary,
   StaticLocal, WriteExitRoutines, WriteDynamicCast, and the eight string-decoding functions.
   k. Builtin registry, 7: gasCPP65Builtins, FindBuiltin, IsBuiltin, BuiltinName, BuiltinOperandType, IsIntrinsic,
   IsInlineMemcpy, FloatBuiltin.
   l. Desugaring with a memo table each: range-for (mcRangeFors), user literals (mcLiteralCalls), brace regrouping

   Clean form: take out the jobs that need only types, tokeniser and context first (b, c, d, g, k), as stage 4 did
   for CCPPUnitData.
   Plan: stage 4's end ("what is left is CCPPUnit: it owns these, walks the declarations and queues the functions")
   is unmet. Jobs b, c, m were stage 6, which was dropped. The rest is new. Progress records "the unit 219
   [functions] and 5592 [lines]" at the checkpoint, which does not match today's size; I did not find out why
   (unconfirmed).
   Confidence: high.
   Since: SemanticLayerPlan (2026-11-22 and 23): the unit's jobs are classes it holds: CCPPDiagnostics (stage 4),
   CCPPConstantFolder (11), CCPPClosures (12), CCPPClassData and CCPPStrings (13), CCPPCheck and CCPPUnitObserver
   (14), CCPPWarnings, CCPPBuiltins, CCPPExceptions, CCPPDesugarings and CCPPInstantiator (15a to 15f), and what an

The IR and the optimiser
------------------------

The IR itself is in good shape. One opcode table (IROpcode.cpp:43 gasIROpcodes) drives the parser, printer and
verifier. Integer constant evaluation is one function (IRFoldInteger) shared by the optimiser's Fold and the
interpreter. Liveness, dominators, loops and the SSA updater are separate small classes. The optimiser is where the
smells are: stage 2 put the passes in ten classes, but each pass still recognises loop counters, loop tests, guards
and "what this branch says" for itself. The opcode table has no columns for the comparison and class facts the
passes need, so they keep private copies. Three analyses answer "how big can this value be", and the pass list is
written out in four code places plus two stale documents.

Answers to the four questions
(a) One table says name, shape, operand kinds, result kind, flags, pure, commutative, may-trap, and each
comparison's inverse, swap and holds. IRParser, IRPrinter and IRVerifier have no ladder over opcodes (the verifier
has one small switch, IRVerifier.cpp:692, for conversion widths). The area has 20 switches over opcodes: IROpcode
2, IRInterpreter 2, IRInterprocedural 2, IRKnownBits 3, Folding 3, Loops 1, Promotion 1, Ranges 5, Verifier 1. I
read 16 of them; IRInterprocedural.cpp:686, IROptimiserPromotion.cpp:540, IROptimiserRanges.cpp:1107 and
IROpcode.cpp:456 I only counted. The ones that restate or disagree with the table are findings 62 and 63.
(b) Integer: one evaluator, used by IROptimiserFolding.cpp:63 and IRInterpreter.cpp:627/657. Float: only the
interpreter evaluates (host float/double, IRInterpreter.cpp:718); the optimiser folds none
(IROptimiserFolding.cpp:56), so nothing can disagree. IRKnownBits does not evaluate; it is one of three bound
calculators (finding 63).
(c) Shared walkers exist (CIRPasses::FirstUse/NextUse/ApplyReplacements, CIRLoops Contains/IsInvariant/GetExits)
but are little used: FirstUse/FirstTemporaryUse have 6 call sites in the IROptimiser*.cpp files, against 35
hand-written block loops and 41 instruction loops. There is no shared walker for counters, loop tests or guards
(findings 60, 61, 65).
(d) A function per idiom. They share pieces (MatchIdiomCounter, MatchIdiomAddress, OnlyLoads, LibraryCall) but each
repeats the shape test and the rewiring (finding 64).

Programs the compiler gets wrong
--------------------------------

Each was compiled as one file by TestCPPConformance's way in (TEST_CPP_CONFORMANCE_TRY) at commit b87a252bc, and by
g++ 15 beside it.  The clause 13 and 14 fixes (18f0f7962, 4f3988433) went in afterwards and touch overloading: none
of these was run again after them.

89. A constant that is 0 but isn't the literal 0 is taken for a null pointer when a call is ranked
   Program:
       int f(char*) { return 1; }
       int f(long long) { return 2; }
       int main() { return f(1 - 1); }
   The compiler, on both processors: "Test.cpp (3, 21): The call is ambiguous [f]".  g++ calls f(long long).
   Cause, by reading: finding 30.  CCPPOverloads::ConversionRank takes any integer constant whose value is 0 for
   a null pointer constant, so f(char*) is a candidate as good as f(long long); the type checker, asked the same
   thing, takes only a literal 0 or nullptr.

90. A function's name ranks as a conversion to void*, which the checker then refuses
   Program:
       void g() {}
       int f(void*) { return 1; }
       int f(...) { return 2; }
       int main() { return f(g); }
   The compiler: "Test.cpp (4, 23): void (*)() becomes void* only by a cast [g]".  g++ calls f(...).
   Cause, by reading: finding 30.  ConversionRank ranks a pointer to a function becoming void* as a conversion,
   so f(void*) wins over the ellipsis; ConvertsPointer then refuses the argument.

91. A return's value is converted by the build and not by the IDE's check
   Program:
       int* f() { return 5; }
       int main() { return 0; }
   A build refuses it on both processors: "Test.cpp (1, 19): Only 0 or nullptr becomes a pointer without a cast
   [5]".  CCPPSemanticPass::Check, which is what the IDE runs, finds nothing wrong with it.
   Cause, by reading: finding 31.  The checker gives a return statement's value only KeepsEnum; the rest of the
   implicit conversion's rules are applied where the IR front end writes the return.

92. Float constants: looked at, and right
   Finding 18 says the float folder has no case for a named cast or ?: where the integer folder has.  This
   program gives the right answer, 8, on the W65C816:
       const float kf = true ? 1.5f : 2.5f;
       float ga[2] = { static_cast<float>(3), kf };
       int main() { static const float s = static_cast<float>(2); return (int)(ga[0] + ga[1] * 2 + s); }
   So those forms are not folded by the float folder and are still initialised rightly (at run time, or by
   another folder; which was not looked into).  Finding 18's point about three evaluators stands; no wrong
   result was found.

Compile time
------------

Measured compiling TestSupportLib65's project for the W65C816 a unit at a time, Release x64
(Tests/CompilerMeasure.py), and by VTune on that compile.  DeclarationsSpeedPlan.txt has the tables.

   CCPP65Builder::Assemble is 15.9% of the compile in the profile of b87a252bc, and the assemble phase 21 s of
   the phases' 120 s after DeclarationsSpeedPlan's stages.  The back ends write assembly as text and the
   assembler parses it; findings 76 and 77 are the same thing seen from the back ends, which read their own text
   back to work on it.

   CCPP65Builder::CompileUnit does everything again for a unit compiled again to inline what was exported after

   away.  Parsing is 24.5 s of the phases' 120 s, both compiles together.  DeclarationsSpeedPlan stage 1 stopped
   the second compile diagnosing; it still reads everything again.

100. Every function of a name in the unit is walked to declare one member
   CCPPUnit::DeclareSignature has three loops over FirstFunctionOfName (every Init and every Kill of every class
   the unit knows) to find the member's own earlier declaration.  Its own time was 2.49% of a compile before
   DeclarationsSpeedPlan stage 3 and 0.57% after, as fewer classes are laid out.  CPPOverloads.cpp has seven
   loops of the same shape.  The functions of a name kept for each class would end the walk; it is worth at
   most 0.6% of a compile now.

   FindMemberTemplate is asked 93,730 times where it was 64,822 on 14 Nov (24,831 on 18 Nov, 73,189 on 20 Nov).
   Quiet trials that fail were 20 on 20 Nov and are 576.  Neither was looked into; the counts are exact, so the
   commit that moved each can be found by measuring.

102. The declarations phase was five times what it had been: fixed
   28.2 s of a 141 s compile, where 5 to 7 s was expected: CCPPUnit::ReportNamedInstances walked every unit's
   whole tree, and CCPPUnit::CheckVirtSpecifiers laid out nearly every class a unit's headers mention.
   Since: DeclarationsSpeedPlan.txt's stages 0 to 3 (2026-11-21); 6.4 s.  TestCPP65Declarations asserts the
   counts, so the same thing coming back fails a test.

103. The IDE's check walks a unit's whole tree to find the types nothing declares
   CCPPUndeclaredTypeFinder (CCPPUnit::ReportUndeclaredTypes) visits every node of the tree, headers and all, in
