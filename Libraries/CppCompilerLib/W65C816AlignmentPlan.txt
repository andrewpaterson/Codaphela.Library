W65C816 alignment plan
======================

Written 2026-11-22.  The user: "Make every type's alignment 1 on the W65C816", then "We're aiming for speed so I'm
happy to keep padding structs to two bytes but alignment can stay at 1 byte", then "Plan capping the padding at 2
bytes and making the alignof 1".  This is the plan only: nothing is built until the user says so.

It closes Cpp14CompliancePlan.txt B 7 (a local's int at an odd address on the W65C816, where alignof(int) is 2).
The 65C64 is not changed.


What is decided
---------------

1. On the W65C816, alignof of every type is 1: a char, an int, a long, a pointer, a double, a class, an array.
   alignas still asks for more and gets it (a class's alignas, a variable's alignas, alignas(n)); alignof(T) of a
   type with an alignas is what alignas asked.  alignas(type) gives alignof(type), so alignas(long) is 1 there.
2. A class's members are placed as now, but at most at a multiple of 2: a member of 2 or more bytes (int, long,
   pointer, float, double, a class with such a member) at an even offset, a char anywhere; a class with such a member
   is a whole number of 2 bytes (trailing padding to 2).  Today they go to multiples of 4 and 8.  This is a default
   #pragma pack(2) for the target: an explicit #pragma pack(1) still packs to 1, and alignas still asks for more.
3. Globals and statics are placed as alignof says (1) but for alignas.  Locals already are (the frame's alignment is
   1, CCPPTargetW65C816::GetFrameAlignment).
4. Why padding is kept: a char followed by padding can later be stored with a 16 bit sta, without sep/rep (6 cycles).
   That optimisation is not part of this plan (step 9 is only a measurement); the 816 has no cost for an odd
   address, so alignof 1 costs nothing.


What it changes, and what may depend on it
------------------------------------------

Structs whose 816 layout changes are those with a member of 4 or 8 bytes after members that don't fill a multiple
of 4 (or 8), and those whose size was padded to 4 or 8: their offsets and sizes shrink.  Looked at so far (2026-11-22):
   - BaseLib's FAT classes: the FAT assembly (Fat*W65C816.asm) takes their offsets from FatW65C816.inc, which
     TestBaseLib65 generates (TestFatW65C816Layout.cpp): regenerate it.  The frame constants (F_*) are the
     assembly's own, not a class's.
   - SKernalInterrupt (KernalInterrupts.asm reads it by offset, 16 bytes a slot): 4, 4, 4, 2, 2 bytes: unchanged.
   - HCP65SFT.h's records (SSFTHeader, SSFTImage, SSFTContainer, SSFTPalette), written by host tools and read on the
     816: unchanged (their uint32 is at 8 either way).
   - BaseLib's SChunkFileHeader: unchanged.
Not looked at yet: everything else.  Step 2 finds them all rather than guessing.


Steps
-----

1. A count, not a change: a switch (TEST_CPP_LAYOUT_CHANGES, or a statistic) makes the W65C816 compiler lay each
   class out both ways and list those whose offsets or size differ (class, file).  Run over every W65C816 compile:
   TestBaseLib65, TestStandardLib65, TestSupportLib65, TestLibc65 and the 20 HCP65 projects.

2. The survey, from that list: for each class that changes, whether anything outside the compiler assumes its
   layout:
   - hand-written assembly (the 29 .asm files: Kernal, Boot, Drivers, Libc65, UserLib, BaseLib's FAT) and any
     .inc of offsets;
   - data that leaves the 816 or comes in as bytes: files written with a struct's bytes and read by a host tool or
     the emulator (or the other way), SD card structures, the executable format, debugger and IDE views that read
     target memory by fixed offsets (HCP65-Emulator, HCP65Lib, HCP65-IDE);
   - static_asserts and tests of sizes or offsets on the W65C816 (TestCPP816*, TestCPPW65C816*, the *65 tests).
   The list goes to the user before step 3.

3. The target says it: CCPPTarget gains what the layout and alignof ask (a member alignment limit, 0 for none and 2
   on the W65C816; and whether alignof is 1).  CCPPClassLayout takes the limit as it takes #pragma pack
   (PackAlignment), so a class's miAlign (used to place it as a member) is at most 2.  CCPPClassLayout::TypeAlignment
   splits in two: the layout's (the type's own, capped) and the object's (1 on the W65C816 but for alignas, the
   type's own on the 65C64).  Its users move to the right one:
   - CCPPClassLayout (fields, bases, virtual bases, the class's own alignment and size): the layout's;
   - alignof (CPPUnitExpressions), alignas(type) (AlignasAlignment), VariableAlignment (globals, statics, locals
     with alignas): the object's.
   The semantic pass checks with the W65C816 target too, so the IDE says the same.

4. Libc65: max_align_t is long long, so alignof(max_align_t) is 1 on the 816: nothing to change, but checked.
   __STDCPP_DEFAULT_NEW_ALIGNMENT__ (C++17's) is left.  The allocator's own alignment is not changed.

5. What the survey found that depends on a layout: fixed (regenerated .inc files; constants; host mirrors),
   each with a test that fails if the two drift apart, as TestFatW65C816Layout does for the FAT classes.

6. Tests:
   - TestCPPW65C816: sizes, offsets and alignof on the 816 (struct { char c; long l; } is 6 bytes with l at 2,
     struct { char c; } 1, struct { char a; short b; char c; } 6, alignof(long) 1, alignas(8) still 8, #pragma pack(1)
     still 1), and the same source on the 65C64 unchanged.
   - Conformance: the cases are the same on both targets and the host, so they compare sizes with sizeof rather
     than numbers; any that compare an alignment with a number are looked at (BasicAlignment, AlignofRules,
     BasicAlignasObjects).  A case that a local int's address is a multiple of alignof(int) on both targets.

7. Docs: HCP65's language.md says what the W65C816's alignment and layout are.

8. Verify as always: TestCppCompilerLib, the three *65 library tests on both targets, TestLibc65, the 20 HCP65
   projects; HCP65Emulator and the IDE if the survey touched them.

9. Afterwards, a measurement only: how many byte stores in the libraries and HCP65 have padding after them, and the
   cycles a 16 bit store there would save, for the user to decide on that optimisation.
